The ratio of NH3 to NH4+ in water at any given time will depend on the pH of the water and the temperature. At pH 7.0 or below, most ammonia (>95%) will be in the non-toxic form (NH4+). This proportion of non-toxic to toxic ammonia will increase greatly as pH increases. Water temperature will also affect the ratio of NH3 to NH4+, with more toxic NH3 present at any given pH in warmer water than in cooler water. For example, at 82°F, the percentage of ammonia that is in the toxic form (NH3) is 2% at pH 7.5, compared to 18% when pH is 8.5. The sum of the gaseous toxic form and the non-toxic ionic form of ammonia is called Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN). TAN is what most commercial ammonia test kits measure.
