# Grower-reported DIY / cheaper alternatives to purpose-built aquaponics and hydroponics equipment, and what fails

Date read for every source below: 2026-11-25. Feeds the "alternatives", "trade-offs" and "failure_modes" fields of an equipment inventory. It is not a farm design. Nothing here is a claim of fish safety, aeration adequacy, solids-removal capacity, profitability or viability.

How to read the labels:
- **Tier.** tier_1 is peer-reviewed. tier_2 is extension, government or institute guidance (including USDA project reports and ICAR magazine articles). tier_3 is forum, blog, vendor, video or news. Every tier_3 claim is an **anecdote**, not established practice.
- **Reporters.** "Independent reporters" counts distinct named or numbered posters or sites that I actually read. Several posters in one thread are counted as people, but the thread is still one site. Unread search hits are never counted.
- **Corroboration.** corroborated means 2 or more independent sources agree. The other values are single_source and conflicting.
- **Session limit.** The shared WebFetch budget for this session (100 fetches per hour, shared by all agents) ran out partway through. Many relevant pages were found by search but could not be read. They are listed under each Gaps heading as "found, not read" and contribute no claims or counts.
- **Prices.** Prices are as stated by each source, with the currency the source gives. Nothing is converted.

## 1. Fish tanks and sumps: what replaces purpose-built tanks (IBC totes, drums, tarpaulin tanks) and what fails?

### Takeaway
Repurposed 1,000 L / 275 US gal IBC totes are the most widely documented substitute. They appear in an extension build guide, the FAO manual's figures and several forums. The dominant warning is previous contents (chemical residue, porous polyethylene). Outdoor service-life anecdotes conflict: about 5 years in direct light versus about 8 years outdoors. No source I read reported bulging. In India, PVC-tarpaulin biofloc tanks are the cheap substitute. The read sources document farmer losses but not tank-specific failure rates.

### Cited Findings

**Alternative 1.1: IBC tote as the fish tank, or cut down as a grow bed** (replaces a purpose-built polyethylene or fibreglass fish tank or grow bed)
- **Cost.** A "Repurposed food grade 275-gallon" IBC is costed at **$65.00 (USD, "Approx., 2023")** in a University of Maryland Extension low-cost build. The whole build is stated as $405.35, but its line items sum to $409.35, so the published total appears inconsistent. Tier_2. — [UMD Extension EM-2023-0698](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Cost (anecdote).** Food-grade totes sell at a premium (posters John Young and Douglas Alpenstock). Dan Boone got a cheap ex-car-wash-soap tote in a bulk buy. No price figures are given. Tier_3. — [Permies "IBC totes" thread](https://permies.com/t/159334/IBC-totes)
- **Why chosen.** UMD uses one tote as both fish tank and grow bed. FAO Technical Paper 589 shows "Example of a newly assembled media bed unit using intermediate bulk containers" (Fig. 4.2, p. 36) and "Media bed unit constructed from intermediate bulk containers" (Fig. 4.51, p. 55). Only the captions were readable, not the body text. Tier_2. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698); [FAO FIAT 589 (Somerville et al. 2014)](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)
- **Failure/warning: previous contents.** UMD says clean with dish soap and water inside and out so "there are no trace materials from the previous cargo that can harm the fish." Its parts list specifies a food-grade IBC. Tier_2. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Failure/warning: previous contents (anecdote, 7 of 8 distinct posters raised it).** The thread has 8 distinct posters, undated beyond "5 years ago".
  - Douglas Alpenstock: "Polyethylene is quite porous, so beware if it held nasty stuff."
  - Phil Grady and Matt Todd: only buy totes with the original contents label, and use the SDS to identify hazards.
  - Kenneth Elwell had totes that held sodium lauryl/laureth sulfate, which he called "harmful to aquatic life". He would use those only for greenhouse thermal mass, not aquaponics.
  - John Young: skip totes that smell of harsh chemicals.
  - Chris Kott raised microplastic concerns and proposed an untested clay or biochar coating.
  - Dan Boone uses totes for aquaponics. He doubts leaching is "biologically significant" once biology establishes, but would not store drinking water in his ex-soap tote.
  - Tier_3. — [Permies "IBC totes"](https://permies.com/t/159334/IBC-totes)
- **Failure/warning: UV, sunlight and service life (anecdote, at least 2 posters on one site, figures conflict).** The thread is "275 and larger IBC's - how long do they last?". No dates or locations are given beyond the opener.
  - One unnamed poster: totes "last about 5 years in direct light", and much longer indoors or under a tarp.
  - Another unnamed poster: clean totes outdoors for about 8 years, "still tough". This poster has about 30 totes on site and notes that some totes have a galvanized metal side liner for sun and puncture protection.
  - A third post (possibly the same person) reports clear, unlined totes in service for about 8 years, with tops covered.
  - The opener (newbury, Vermont/Virginia/Mississippi) worried about brittleness in sub-zero winters and breakdown within about 2 years. That is a worry, not a report.
  - Tier_3. — [TractorByNet IBC lifespan thread](https://tractorbynet.com/forums/goto/post?id=2710792)
- **Failure/warning: algae and light.** UMD's optional step for outdoor systems is to spray flat or matte paint on the exterior, which "will eliminate algae from growing in the tanks". UMD does not discuss UV degradation of the plastic. Tier_2. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Failure/warning: weight.** The filled tank weighs "up to 2,330 lbs", so it should sit on a weight-bearing floor. Tier_2. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Failure/warning: bulging and cage rust.** Not found in any source I read.
- **Region.** US (Maryland extension; US forums). FAO is global.
- **Reliability.**
  - Previous-contents warning: tier_2 + tier_3, **corroborated** (UMD + 7 Permies posters). Confidence **medium**: one extension source agrees with many anecdotes, but no residue-testing study was found.
  - Outdoor service life: tier_3, **conflicting** (5 vs about 8 years, one site). Confidence **low**: anecdotes, no exposure conditions, one thread.

**Alternative 1.2: white polyethylene drum as a fish tank**
- FAO shows "A 1 000 litre fish tank made from a white polyethylene drum" (Fig. 4.22, p. 43). Only the caption was readable. Tier_2. Single_source. Confidence **low**: no body text, cost or failure data. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)

**Alternative 1.3: PVC-tarpaulin (biofloc) tank** (India; replaces cement or FRP tanks)
- **Farmer outcomes, West Bengal.** This peer-reviewed survey covered 110 biofloc farmers, randomly sampled from a list of 390 across 13 districts, interviewed in late 2020.
  - "Disease outbreaks and mass mortalities were very common", and prevention and treatment were rated "quite unsatisfactory".
  - Satisfaction: 13.6% highly satisfied, 62.7% dissatisfied but continuing, 23.7% highly disappointed.
  - The main technical gap was failing to synchronize the C:N ratio with microbial dynamics.
  - The abstract page does not give tank type, costs, power-outage data or abandonment counts.
  - Tier_1 (Mali et al., J. Inland Fish. Soc. India 53(3-4): 132-142, 2021). — [ICAR ePubs JIFSI](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JIFSI/article/view/122360)
- **Expert warning, Tamil Nadu (ICAR-CIBA, Chennai).** Principal Scientist Kumaraguru Vasagam said many farmers "burnt their fingers" after being "lured by videos on YouTube", and that ICAR-CIBA has received reports of losses from improper implementation. Director K.K. Vijayan called biofloc "a double-edged sword". The article names Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Delhi as places where farmers use it. It gives no loss numbers and no tank type. Tier_3 (IANS news, 18 Nov 2020) quoting tier_2 institute scientists. — [Sambad English / IANS](https://sambadenglish.com/aquaculture-farmers-should-use-biofloc-technology-carefully)
- **Reliability.** "Indian biofloc farmers frequently report losses or problems" is **corroborated** (tier_1 survey + institute scientists via news). Confidence **medium**: the survey is from West Bengal, not the four target southern states, and neither source isolates the tarpaulin tank as the cause. Tarpaulin tank lifespan, tears and leaks: **not found** in sources read.

### Inferences
- The IBC appeals because it is cheap, available and food-grade. Both the extension and the forum sources put the main risk on unknown previous contents, not on the tote design. An inventory should record "previous contents / label / SDS" as a procurement check.
- The IBC service-life anecdotes cannot be reconciled without exposure data: sun hours, whether the cage has a metal liner, cover or paint. Treat them as a range of anecdotes, not a lifetime figure.
- The biofloc evidence points mostly at management (C:N, disease) rather than tank hardware. The tarpaulin tank cannot be credited or blamed from what was read.

### Gaps
- IBC bulging, cage corrosion and outlet-valve leaks: not found in sources read.
- No study measuring leaching from repurposed IBCs into aquaculture water was found.
- Tarpaulin tank cost (INR), GSM rating, lifespan and failure modes: found but not read: [indbiofloc blog "Cons"](https://indbiofloc.blogspot.com/search/label/Cons), [bioflocindia blog on tarpaulin tanks](https://bioflocindia.blogspot.com/2019/11/biofloc-fish-farming-tarpaulin-tank.html), [differ.blog tarpaulin DIY guide](https://differ.blog/p/biofloc-tank-diy-guide-to-using-tarpaulin-the-right-way-0c7b7c), [NFDB biofloc booklet](https://nfdb.gov.in/PDF/Biofloc%20booklet%20v6.pdf) (robots.txt unreachable), [CIFE tarpaulin tank note 2023](https://www.cife.edu.in/pdf/News/Tarpaulin%20tank-based%20fish%20farming-6-9-2023.pdf), [Kerala biofloc economic evaluation (JIFA)](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JIFA/article/view/139820).
- No Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh or Telangana-specific grower reports were read.
- Netherlands/EU grower reports on tank substitutes: not found.
- The full text of Love et al. 2015 would be needed for any equipment or problem data. The abstract page gives only the sample: 257 respondents, 81% US. — [JHSPH CLF](https://clf.jhsph.edu/publications/commercial-aquaponics-production-and-profitability-findings-international-survey)

## 2. Grow units: media beds, rafts (EPS/XPS boards), NFT from gutters or PVC: what replaces purpose-built units and what fails?

### Takeaway
Homemade EPS rafts cut from insulation board, media-filled totes and PVC or square-pipe NFT channels are all documented substitutes. The strongest evidence is on rafts. A USDA-funded project found that homemade EPS rafts absorbed water (weight up more than 300%) and broke at a 5.9 kg load. It found coliforms and generic E. coli on unsanitised rafts. It judged XPS unsuitable for its raft manufacture because of mandatory fire retardants. Food-safety opinion on polystyrene is split between regulators' food-contact approval and the precautionary concern about styrene.

### Cited Findings

**Alternative 2.1: insulation-board EPS or XPS cut as DWC rafts** (replaces purpose-made food-grade raft boards)
- **Breakage and water uptake.** In Hawaii Fish Company's USDA NIFA SBIR project (Phase I, 2017-2020, with USDA ARS Hilo), homemade EPS rafts showed "increasing over 300% in weight and exhibiting a maximum loading rate of only 5.9 kg (13 lbs) before breaking". Homemade EPS had the highest water absorption after one simulated production cycle. Molded EPS of similar or higher density absorbed less. All rafts lost freeboard under an 8.16 kg simulated load. Tier_2 (USDA project report, not peer reviewed). — [USDA NIFA CRIS project 1012572](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html)
- **Food safety: microbial.** "Total coliforms and generic E. coli were present in countable numbers" on top and submerged raft surfaces before sanitation. A chlorine rinse "largely eliminated the generic E. coli", and 48 h of desiccation reduced bacterial counts and ATP. Tier_2. — [USDA NIFA CRIS 1012572](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html)
- **Flame retardant (XPS).** XPS rafts performed better on absorption and load, but "due to the mandatory fire retardants, are not suitable for planned aquaponic raft manufacture". Tier_2. — [USDA NIFA CRIS 1012572](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html)
- **Food safety: styrene (blog Q&A, anecdote/opinion).**
  - NonTox U (Lisa Powers, 2019, updated Dec 2020) says FDA and EFSA "consider polystyrene safe for use in contact with food", citing chemicalsafetyfacts.org.
  - It also says the EPA considers styrene "a probable human carcinogen". The author personally avoids polystyrene in food growing.
  - It notes that Greenguard certification on some XPS does not cover leaching into food or water. It says some polyurethane floats contain isocyanurates, citing a 2011 365aquaponics blog.
  - No primary studies are cited, and flame retardants are not mentioned.
  - Tier_3. — [NonTox U "Styrofoam in Hydroponics"](https://nontox.squarespace.com/qa-posts/styrofoam-in-hydroponics)
- **Use is documented.** FAO shows "Swiss chard (Beta sp.) suspended on a polystyrene raft in a deep water culture canal" (Fig. 4.39) and "Polystyrene sheet in a small deep water culture unit showing planting holes" (Fig. 4.74). Captions only. Tier_2. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)
- **Cost.** Not found in sources read.
- **Reliability.**
  - Breakage and water uptake of homemade EPS: tier_2, **single_source**, confidence **medium**. It is a measured test, but one unreviewed project report.
  - XPS fire-retardant concern: tier_2 single_source. The tier_3 blog neither confirms nor contradicts it.
  - Styrene food safety: **conflicting** (regulatory food-contact approval as relayed by the blog, versus the EPA carcinogen classification the same blog cites). Confidence **low**: no primary sources were read.

**Alternative 2.2: media bed (gravel, expanded clay, shale or lava rock in a tote) as combined solids filter, biofilter and grow unit** (replaces a separate clarifier, biofilter and grow unit)
- **Why chosen.** UMD uses clay pebbles, expanded shale or volcanic rock as "grow media and biofilter" in one IBC, with a bell siphon. Hydroton cost: 4 × 50 L bags at $37.36 each, $149.44 (USD, 2023). Tier_2. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Failure: clogging and anaerobic zones (anecdote).** Murray Hallam (Australia) dug out a 5-year-old gravel bed after its pH "stopped dropping and began rising". He suspected "one or two 'dead' areas".
  - The page cites a "commonly held estimate" of about 18 months before maintenance is needed without supplementary solids filtration, depending on fish load and bed size.
  - Compost worms extend bed life for many enthusiasts but "are not the entire solution".
  - Replacing the media with 3/4-inch gravel took about 2 hours with an assistant. Hallam considers digging out every 5 years acceptable.
  - Counterpoint from commenter "Frank": a 100% hydroton system was still fine after more than 2 years.
  - Separately, a pump failure came from an uncleaned pump filter screen.
  - Tier_3, 2 reporters on one site. — [ecofilms.com.au "When growbeds get old"](https://www.ecofilms.com.au/aquaponics-when-growbeds-get-old-sht-happens/)
- **Failure mechanism (extension).** Oklahoma State: "If solids accumulate, then denitrification can occur, which will result in ammonia concentrations increasing". Denitrification can produce hydrogen sulfide, which the fact sheet calls "toxic for fish". Its maintenance list includes "Clear root obstructions from pipes or drains in beds". It does not discuss worms or channeling. Tier_2. — [OSU "Nitrification and Maintenance in Media Bed Aquaponics"](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/nitrification-and-maintenance-in-media-bed-aquaponics.html)
- **Worms.** FAO shows "Redworms (Eisenia fetida) from a vermicompost unit" (Fig. 9.2). The body text was not read. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)
- **Reliability.** "Solids accumulation in media beds creates anaerobic zones and needs periodic clean-out" is **corroborated** (tier_3 Hallam + tier_2 OSU mechanism). Confidence **medium**. The 18-month and 5-year intervals are tier_3 single_source, confidence **low**.

**Alternative 2.3: PVC pipe, square pipe or rain gutter as NFT channels** (replaces purpose-made NFT channels)
- FAO: "Lettuce growing in square pipes of a nutrient film technique unit" (Fig. 4.38). Caption only. Tier_2. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)
- India: The Better India (12 Dec 2019) gives a DIY guide built from PVC pipes, elbows, a container and a submersible pump. It states no cost. Its only warning is that the pump runs continuously, so connect to power backup. It does not discuss heat, algae or pipe colour. Tier_3. — [The Better India](https://www.thebetterindia.com/205495/how-to-build-hydroponics-unit-pvc-pipes-cheap-veggies-soilless-farming-india/)
- Reliability: use is documented, but failure data are **not found** in sources read. Confidence **low**.

### Inferences
- Water uptake and low breaking load in cut EPS imply a shorter raft life and more breakage than molded rafts. No lifetime figure was read, so lifetime must stay missing.
- Because XPS insulation is sold with flame retardants, at least one USDA-funded developer treated it as unsuitable. An inventory should record the flame-retardant question as an open food-contact issue for any insulation board.
- chemicalsafetyfacts.org, the source the NonTox blog relays, is to my knowledge run by an industry body (American Chemistry Council). I did not verify this in this session, so treat the "FDA/EFSA consider safe" relay with care and check the regulators directly.

### Gaps
- Raft cost (cut insulation board vs purpose-made), raft lifetime, root intrusion and fragment shedding: not found.
- The HBCD or other specific flame retardant in XPS or EPS boards sold in the EU or India: not found.
- Rain-gutter NFT failure reports (heat gain, flat-bottom ponding, root dams): found, not read: [THCFarmer "$200 NFT rain gutter system"](https://www.thcfarmer.com/threads/my-200-00-nft-rain-gutter-system.60308/page-4?amp=1), [Hawaii STEM forum "NFT (PVC Pipe OR Rain Gutters)"](https://stempreacademy.hawaii.edu/groups/steamer/forum/discussion/nft-pvc-pipe-or-rain-gutters), [Science in Hydroponics on NFT](https://scienceinhydroponics.com/?p=3734), [Oregon State EM9457 NFT](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em9457), [Virginia Tech SPES-463](https://pubs.ext.vt.edu/SPES/spes-463/spes-463.html).
- Indian PVC/UPVC NFT channel prices on IndiaMART were found but not read (vendor listings).

Extension papers (Texas A&M/extension.org, Ron) and forum threads on airlifts exist, but the fetch budget ran out before they could be read. ## 4. Aeration: cheap aquarium air pumps and DIY backup: what is reported?

### Takeaway
The extension build uses an aquarium air pump and air stone, and its own sizing is inconsistent. Power loss stopping air or water flow is the most-repeated failure, across extension, Indian media and an Australian blog. These sources describe the failure mode only. None of them establishes aeration adequacy.

### Cited Findings
- **Aquarium air pump instead of a blower.** UMD's text says to use "an air pump for a 250 to 300-gallon system" for a 275 gal IBC, but its cost table lists "Air Pump (150 gallons)" at $74.60 (USD, 2023). It names Tetra Whisper and AQUANEAT options, an air stone at $6.00 and tubing at $3.00. It says to keep the pump on a shelf away from water. Tier_2. The source contradicts itself on sizing. — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/how-build-low-cost-small-scale-aquaponic-system-em-2023-0698)
- **Failure: power or pump loss.** OSU lists power or pump failure as making dissolved oxygen decrease, and leaks as draining all water, "stressing and killing the fish and plants". Tier_2. — [OSU media-bed fact sheet](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/nitrification-and-maintenance-in-media-bed-aquaponics.html)
- **Failure: accidental unplugging (anecdote, second-hand).** An Australian blog recounts that a child unplugged the main pump at Christmas and "the fish were all dead by the next morning". It also recounts a person who unplugged the aerator for a power tool and forgot it. Tier_3, 1 site reporting 2 second-hand cases. — [ecofilms.com.au "Battery Backup in Aquaponics"](https://www.ecofilms.com.au/?p=3803)
- **Reliability.** "Loss of power to air or water pumps is a primary failure mode" is **corroborated** (tier_2 OSU + tier_3 ecofilms + tier_3 Better India, Section 13). Confidence **medium**.

### Inferences
- Mismatched sizing in a published extension build suggests that aquarium-pump sizing labels ("for X gallons") are a weak basis for an inventory. Record the label as stated, without treating it as adequacy.

### Gaps
- Aquarium air pump service life, diaphragm failure and noise reports: not found.
- Venturi or paddlewheel substitutes in Indian biofloc: not read.

## 5. Solids removal: barrel swirl filters and radial flow separators instead of drum filters: what is reported?

### Takeaway
The best evidence is a peer-reviewed comparison at commercial coldwater RAS scale. A radial-flow settler removed 77.9% of TSS against 37.1% for a swirl separator, and the authors still judged a full-flow microscreen drum filter "critical". That study does not test DIY barrel versions at backyard scale. DIY barrel builds were found but not read.

### Cited Findings
- **Swirl vs radial-flow settler (tier_1 study, via trade summary).**
  - Davidson & Summerfelt (Conservation Fund Freshwater Institute, *Aquacultural Engineering* 2005) report mean TSS removal of **37.1 ± 3.3%** for the swirl separator and **77.9 ± 1.6%** for the radial-flow settler, "highly significant".
  - Daily TSS mass removed was about 23% for swirl and about 48% for radial.
  - Both settlers ran at a surface loading of 0.0031 m³/s per m². They treated the 7-8% of flow leaving the bottom-centre drain of a 150 m³ dual-drain tank with trout and char, at feed up to 123 kg/day.
  - A microscreen drum filter on the full flow removed 40-45% of daily TSS mass. The authors conclude that treating the entire flow with a drum filter is "critical… regardless of which settler is used".
  - Tier_1, single study, confidence **medium**: one study, at a scale and species far from backyard tilapia.
  - [GSA Global Aquaculture Advocate summary](https://www.globalseafood.org/advocate/settler-evaluation-radial-flow-vs-tangential-flow/)
- **Mechanical separator in FAO.** FAO shows mechanical separators including one with baffles (Figs. 4.25-4.27, p. 45). Captions only. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)

### Inferences

## 10. Structures: bamboo, wooden or GI-frame polyhouses (India): what is reported?

### Takeaway
Indian institute guidance documents bamboo and eucalyptus-pole greenhouses as low-cost alternatives to steel structures. Treated bamboo is quoted at 8-10 years. The read sources record these failure modes: termites, film damage from sharp points and wood resin, wind, mild-steel rust, and dust cutting light by up to 15%. No INR cost figures were found in the sources read.

### Cited Findings
- **Eucalyptus or casuarina pole and bamboo greenhouse (Tamil Nadu, Chennai).**
  - Shri AMM Murugappa Chettiar Research Centre (MCRC, Taramani) specifies, for a 35 × 20 ft (700 sq ft) house: 55 poles, 30 bamboo sticks, 4 mm GI wire, coal tar or bitumen against termites, and about 1,736 sq ft of 200-micron UV-stabilised LDPE film.
  - Warnings: sharp pole points damage the film, so wrap them; wood resin migration degrades the film; plastic rope holds the sheet against wind.
  - The document claims "Long life" without figures and gives no cost. It is internally inconsistent on eucalyptus versus casuarina.
  - Tier_2 (research centre, hosted on the Government of India Vikaspedia portal), single_source, confidence **medium** for materials and **low** for lifespan.
  - [Vikaspedia MCRC PDF](https://static.vikaspedia.in/media/files_en/agriculture/farm-based-enterprises/cost-effective-wooden-structured-greenhouse.pdf)
- **Bamboo lifespan and steel rust (Northeast India).**
  - "If treated well, these Bamboo structures can sustain 8 to 10 years". Bamboo is treated with turpentine and tar.
  - For mild steel polyhouses: "paint the structure with silver paint once in every two years" to avoid rust.
  - "Accumulation of dust particles on the top of plastic reduces the light transmissions in the polyhouses up to 15%."
  - No costs are given.
  - Tier_2 (ICAR *Indian Horticulture* magazine, Zambre, Nov-Dec 2023), single_source, confidence **medium**.
  - [ICAR ePubs](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/download/148520/53882/472661)
- **Reliability.** Bamboo/wood as a low-cost structure option is **corroborated** (two independent Indian institute sources, Tamil Nadu and Northeast). The 8-10 year lifespan is single_source.

### Inferences
- Both sources put the life-limiting factors in treatment and maintenance: tar and bitumen, wrapping, and periodic painting of steel. An inventory "failure_modes" field should hold termite/rot, film abrasion and corrosion, not a single lifetime figure.

### Gaps
- INR per m² costs for bamboo vs GI vs MS structures, and state-specific (KA/TN/AP/TS) grower experiences: not found in sources read. Found, not read: [agrifarming.in polyhouse cost/subsidy](https://www.agrifarming.in/polyhouse-subsidy-cost-profit-report), [USQ/ACIAR LWR polyhouse report 2019](https://dsi4mtf.unisq.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/No-16-LWR-Project-Polyhouse-report-31.01.2019.pdf), [Vigyan Ashram Protected Cultivation Manual 2025](https://vigyanashram.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Protected-Cultivation-Manual-vFinal.pdf), [ICAR Indian Horticulture article 120985](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/download/120985/46378/472914).

## 13. Power backup: solar plus battery, car battery with DC pump, UPS, generator: what is reported?

### Takeaway
One Australian blogger documents a cheap DIY backup: a 12 V car battery trickle-charged by a 5 W solar panel (about $60 on eBay, currency not stated), with a relay that switches a 12 V bilge pump on when mains fails. The old battery lasted only about 2 h. The same author still calls a petrol generator the best investment. Indian DIY guidance also says to connect continuously running pumps to backup. No UPS-for-air-pump reports were read.

### Cited Findings
- **12 V battery, relay and bilge pump (replaces a mains-only system or a generator).**
  - A relay keeps 12 V off while mains is present and closes on mains failure to run a 12 V bilge pump that circulates and splashes water.
  - The battery is charged by a 5 W panel on a west-facing wall that gets about 1 h of sunset sun. The battery holds about 13.9 V, which the author says is enough to run all night.
  - The old battery ran out after about 2 h.
  - Cost: "about $60" for a small battery-charging solar panel on eBay. The currency is not stated; the site is Australian.
  - Warnings: batteries self-discharge, so recharge them; overcharging can boil a battery dry; an untested backup may lack charge; check with a multimeter. The author recommends a portable petrol generator as "the best investment for peace of mind".
  - Tier_3 (ecofilms.com.au, author not shown, undated), single_source, confidence **low**.
  - [ecofilms.com.au "Battery Backup in Aquaponics"](https://www.ecofilms.com.au/?p=3803)
- **India.** The Better India says that because the pump runs continuously with no timer, the system should be connected to power backup. Tier_3. — [The Better India](https://www.thebetterindia.com/205495/how-to-build-hydroponics-unit-pvc-pipes-cheap-veggies-soilless-farming-india/)
- **Solar-powered pump documented.** FAO shows "Photovoltaic cells used to power a water pump" (Fig. 9.10). Caption only. Tier_2. — [FAO FIAT 589](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2ca21047-390f-42cd-bd1d-0c2ebc9c1df2/content)
- **Reliability.** "Growers add backup for pumps and aeration" is **corroborated** (ecofilms + Better India + OSU on the failure mode). The specific battery runtimes are single_source, confidence **low**.

### Inferences
- The one DIY battery report shows the main risk is an untested or depleted battery, not the concept. An inventory "failure_modes" entry should include "backup battery self-discharge / not tested".

### Gaps
- UPS run-times for aquarium air pumps; solar plus battery sizing reports from India (frequent outages); generator vs battery costs in INR or EUR. Found, not read: [Go Green Aquaponics power-failure blog (vendor)](https://gogreenaquaponics.com/blogs/news/how-to-protect-your-aquaponics-system-during-power-failure), [Aquarium Co-Op power outage guide](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/power-outage-preparation), [Aquarium Co-Op forum "power failure… everything works as planned"](https://forum.aquariumcoop.com/topic/38950-power-failure-meh-when-everything-works-as-planned).

## 16. Harvest and cold storage: CoolBot cold rooms and chest-freezer conversions: what is reported?

### Takeaway
DIY CoolBot coolers, a window AC with a CoolBot controller in an insulated box, are documented by two independent US extension sources. One farm build cost $1,300 USD. The University of Minnesota lists controller $374 and AC $499-$769, and complete units from $8,114 to $19,672. Limitations include small and well-insulated rooms only, infrequent opening, no large warm loads, AC compatibility, the need for a floor, and outdoor-rated AC outdoors. Chest-freezer conversions were not read.

### Cited Findings
- **CoolBot on a shoestring (Vermont).**
  - All One Farm, Putney VT, a salad-greens farm under 1 acre, built a 6 × 8 ft walk-in from five portable polyiso and OSB panels rated "25+ R-value". The stated total was **$1,300 (USD)** including the CoolBot and a 12,000 BTU AC. No line items are given.
  - Insulation: polyiso 2-inch at about $35/sheet; 4-inch under $80/sheet suggested.
  - Lessons learned: test-fit the foam; close the door before foaming its perimeter, because expanding foam warped the frame on this build.
  - No failure, icing or humidity data and no lifetime are given.
  - Tier_2 (UVM Extension case study), confidence **medium**.
  - [UVM Extension Produce Safety Portal](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/produceportal/case-studies/coolbot-shoestring)
- **CoolBot costs and limits (Minnesota, 15 Nov 2024).**
  - Costs: CoolBot controller **$374**; 14,000 BTU 115 V LG AC **$499**; 24,500 BTU 220 V LG AC **$769**.
  - Complete units (controller, new AC, box, floor, shipping estimate): **$8,114** (8 × 8 ft indoor, 120 V) to **$19,672** (10 × 20 ft outdoor, 220 V). The 10 × 20 ft indoor unit is printed as "$17.625", likely a typo for $17,625.
  - Limits: best for coolers under 10 × 20 ft that are well insulated, opened infrequently and not loaded with large volumes of warm product. Not all ACs are compatible. Without a floor, performance drops significantly. Outdoor use needs an "outdoor" model. Larger units need 220 V and possibly an electrician. Retrofitting a used box saves money but adds significant labour.
  - Recommended for farmers on rented land because the units are movable.
  - Tier_2, confidence **medium**.
  - [UMN Extension Fruit & Vegetable News](https://blog-fruit-vegetable-ipm.extension.umn.edu/2024/11/coolbot-walk-in-coolers.html)
- **Reliability.** CoolBot use by small farms is **corroborated** (UVM + UMN, both tier_2). Costs differ because the scope differs: a DIY panel build versus purchased complete units. These are context differences, not a conflict.

### Gaps
- CoolBot failure modes (evaporator icing, AC compressor wear, warranty) are not discussed in either source.
- Chest-freezer-to-fridge conversions (external thermostat): not read.
- CoolBot or AC-based cold rooms in India (power quality, ambient 35-40 °C): not found.
- Found, not read: [UKY "How to Build a Walk-In Cooler"](https://marketready.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/How-to-Build-a-Walk-In-Cooler-for-Your-Small-Farm.pdf), [Practical Farmers of Iowa cooler analysis](https://practicalfarmers.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/14.OE_.H.cooler-analysis1-1.pdf), [Cornell Small Farms CoolBot](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/2012/06/coolbot-enables-small-farmers-to-build-do-it-yourself-coolers), [K-State MF3441](https://bookstore.ksre.ksu.edu/pubs/MF3441.pdf).
