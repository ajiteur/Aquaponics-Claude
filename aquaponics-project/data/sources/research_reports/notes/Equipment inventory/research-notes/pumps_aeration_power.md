# Aquaponics/hydroponics equipment inventory: water pumps, aeration and backup oxygen, power

Scope note: an evidence-first inventory for a component database, not a farm design. Date read for every web value: 2026-11-25. Tiers: tier_1 = peer-reviewed, government, university research station (UVI, SRAC); tier_2 = extension guides, research-institute notes, manufacturer datasheets/web stores (for specs), university theses; tier_3 = retail/B2B listings, forums, blogs. FAO Technical Paper 589 is recorded as tier_1 (intergovernmental technical paper); a reviewer may reclassify it as tier_2 (extension manual). No currency conversion is done anywhere in these notes. No value is estimated. "not found" = searched for and not found; "not retrieved" = a source was located but could not be opened (see constraint). Nothing here is a claim of fish safety, aeration adequacy, oxygen-transfer capacity, profitability or viability.

Tag format used on each value: `[tier | confidence: level, reason | corroboration]`.

**Research constraint (read first):** the shared WebFetch budget (100 fetches per hour, shared by all parallel agents) was exhausted early in this run and stayed exhausted for most of it; direct downloads (curl) are blocked by the proxy, and search returned only titles and URLs, never page text. ## Q1. Inventory records: item, function, system/grow-method/scale fit, capacity, power, price, failure mode

### Takeaway
Eighteen item types were recorded. Sizing rules and failure behaviour are documented in tier_1 sources (FAO 589, SRAC 453/454, UVI). Datasheet curves, lifetimes and prices are thin. Verified prices cover NL air pumps and one Oase water pump (toppy.nl), a 2020 Honda Benelux generator and engine-pump list, one Indian pump (Crompton), one IndiaMART aerator category page, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy rates. **WP-5 Airlift pump (alternative to water pump)**
- FAO 589: airlifts "can be more electrically efficient, but only at small head heights (30-40 cm)" and are "best at a depth greater than one metre" — [FAO 589 sec. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | corroborated in direction by SRAC 453 below]`
- SRAC 453: airlifts need about 4 cfm of air per lb of daily feed (half drives the lift, the rest feeds diffuser hoses); they can supply all aeration below 1/4 lb/gal fish density and about half the oxygen demand at 1/2 lb/gal; airlifts over 8 in (20 cm) diameter "can move several hundred gpm" at lifts "<18 inches (46 cm)"; airlifts are described as energy efficient — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | corroborated (FAO 589 agrees airlifts suit low lift)]`
- Measured example (raft system, 300+ gal): eliminating the water pump with an airlift gave a stated 51% energy reduction; the airlift moved 10 L/min at 25 °C (abstract) versus a stated ~10% under-performance in Ch. III (internal conflict, both recorded); water:air ratio 1:1.5 with air stones, 1:1.85 with tubing only; submergence ratio about 50% (14 in lift, 15 in submerged) — [Olafs 2014, Embry-Riddle M.S. thesis](https://core.ac.uk/download/217154634.pdf) `[tier_2 | confidence: low-medium, single small test system with internal inconsistencies | single_source / conflicting within source]`
- system/grow method/scale: DWC, media bed, small RAS; hobby to small commercial (low-lift layouts). price: not applicable (built from pipe + air pump). failure mode: stops with the air pump; also stops aeration it provides (inference, see Inferences).

**AE-4 Diffusers and air stones (fine-bubble discs, tubes, stones)**
- UVI: each rearing tank "heavily aerated with 22 air diffusers"; hydroponic tanks "numerous small air diffusers (one every 4 feet)"; target DO "near 80 percent saturation (6 to 7 milligrams per liter)" — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | corroborated on spacing intent by FAO 589 DWC rule]`
- FAO DWC: "The air stones should release about 4 liters of air per minute" and "be arranged every 2-4 m2 of canal area" — [FAO 589 DWC section, FarmHub](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/deep-water-culture-technique/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source for the numeric rule]`

**OX-1 Pure oxygen: compressed cylinders and liquid oxygen (LOX)**
- backup options listed: "a backup electrical generator, liquid oxygen tanks, or a mechanical blower", with alarms and auto-dialers; compressed oxygen has unlimited storage life; LOX is perishable but can deliver without power as it warms and vaporizes; pure-oxygen cost depends largely on distance to an industrial oxygen source — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`

## Q4. Sizing rules from UVI, FAO 589, SRAC and other extension sources

### Takeaway
Tier_1 sources give two different bases for circulation sizing. FAO 589 uses fish-tank turnover (2 per hour dense, 1 per hour low density). SRAC 453 uses flow per unit of daily feed (42–84 lpm per kg/day). Aeration is likewise sized per feed (SRAC 453: 3 cfm per lb daily feed) or per stone/area (FAO DWC: about 4 L/min per stone every 2–4 m²). Aqu@teach says there is no single rule of thumb. These are context-dependent rules, recorded side by side, not averaged.

### Cited Findings
- Water turnover: "A commonly cited guideline for densely-stocked aquaponic systems is to cycle the water two times per hour"; at low stocking "the water only needs to be cycled one time per hour" — [FAO 589 sec. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | context_split with Aqu@teach and SRAC 453]`
- Low-density DWC: "ensure that at least half of the total fish tank volume is exchanged per hour"; each canal "has 1-4 hours of retention time" — [FAO 589 DWC section](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/deep-water-culture-technique/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Turnover range: "This can vary from 3 times per hour in very intensive systems to only a few times per day in extensive systems"; "There is no rule of thumb" — [Aqu@teach/FarmHub](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/aquateach/12-design-and-build/connections-water-movement-and-aeration/) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | context_split]`
- Feed-based recirculation: "5 to 10 gallons per minute per pound of daily feed ration (42 to 84 lpm/kg/d)"; design head about 25 ft (8 m) in older designs and about 10 ft (3 m) in modern designs; axial pumps suit lifts under 3 m and are "rarely used unless the system recirculation rates reach several hundred gallons per minute (>2,500 lpm)" — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`
- Feed-based aeration: "3 cubic feet per minute of air is required for each pound of daily food ration"; aeration must replace the system's oxygen "every 20 to 30 minutes" at peak feeding; warm-water systems run at 5–6 ppm DO — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`
- Oxygen demand design value: "the ratio of 1.0 kg of oxygen per 1 kg of feed fed is the safe recommended design value" (citing Timmons & Ebeling) — [Olafs 2014](https://core.ac.uk/download/217154634.pdf) `[tier_2, secondary citation of a tier_2 textbook | confidence: medium | single_source as read]`. Note: per project rules this is an oxygen-demand design ratio, not a total-oxygen-demand or aeration-adequacy claim.
- UVI as built: 1/2 hp pump at 100 gpm for 29,375 gal total water (rearing-tank mean retention 82 minutes); blowers 1.5 hp (fish) + 1 hp (plants); 22 diffusers per 2,060 gal rearing tank; one diffuser every 4 ft in raft tanks; DO near 80% saturation (6–7 mg/L); SRAC 454 says solids removal, pump and blowers "should be sized accordingly" when scaling — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`
- FAO small-unit aeration: 4–8 L/min for all air stones combined for a ~1,000 L fish-tank unit; DWC 4 L/min per stone, one per 2–4 m² — [FAO 589 sec. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/), [FAO 589 DWC](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/deep-water-culture-technique/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Retail pond rule (NL): roughly 2 L/min per 1,000 L at low stocking, 4 L/min per 1,000 L at high stocking (koi) — [toppy.nl category page](https://www.toppy.nl/category/1386/vijver/vijverbeluchting/luchtpomp-vijver.html) `[tier_3 | confidence: low, retailer guidance for ponds, not aquaponics | single_source]`
- DO thresholds: optimum DO for each organism "5–8 mg/litre"; DO 3.0–5.0 ppm is a "12-24 hour range of tolerance / stressful conditions" (FAO Fig. 3.3); nitrification decreases below 2.0 mg/L — [FAO 589 via SlideShare copy](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/un-aquaponics-manual/46368284) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium, read from a third-party copy | corroborated in range by SRAC 454 (6–7 mg/L target) and SRAC 453 (5–6 ppm)]`
- Diffuser depth ≤ about 1.5 m; blower pressure 1.2–1.8 m; airlifts can provide all aeration below 1/4 lb/gal — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`
- Water loss and top-up: fish tanks "will lose between 5-10 cm of water depth that can be stored by the spare volume of the pump sump and biofilter"; top-up "1.5% during normal operation, failures not included" — [Aqu@teach/FarmHub](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/aquateach/12-design-and-build/connections-water-movement-and-aeration/) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | single_source]`

### Inferences
- Turnover-based (FAO) and feed-based (SRAC 453) rules can give very different pump sizes for the same tank, depending on stocking and feed. The component database should store the rule, its basis and its source context (system type, density) and not reduce them to one "L/h per m³" number.
- The FAO small-unit air rule (4–8 L/min for ~1,000 L) and the NL retail pond rule (2–4 L/min per 1,000 L) are of a similar order but come from different contexts (aquaponics unit vs. ornamental pond) and tiers, so they are not corroboration.

### Gaps
- FAO 589 section 9.1.6 (alternative energy) and 9.2 (float switches/backups) were not read.
- No sizing rule was found for NFT, Dutch bucket, tower or ebb-and-flow pump duty (e.g. flow per channel) in the sources read; ebb-and-flow/media-bed timer cycles were not retrieved.
- SRAC 5007 ("Principles of Small-Scale Aquaponics") was located but not read: [SRAC 5007](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf).
- Generator/UPS sizing rules for aquaponics (kVA per kW of pumps and blowers, start-up surge) not found.

## Q5. Reported lifetimes and maintenance

### Takeaway
The only reported pump life figure found is FAO 589's 3–5 years overall, with 1–2 years at full capacity (manual statement, no test basis). Hiblow specifies replacing diaphragms and valves every 1 to 1.5 years. One LiFePO4 datasheet (Victron) rates 2500 cycles at 80 % DoD. Generator service hours, lead-acid life and PV lifetimes were not retrieved. Warranties (Hailea 2 years at toppy.nl; Crompton 1 year) are recorded as warranties, not lifetimes.

### Cited Findings
- Submersible pumps: maintain capacity/efficiency "for least 1-2 years", overall life span "3-5 years"; internal filter cleaning "every 2-3 weeks" — [FAO 589 sec. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium, basis not stated | single_source]`
- Air stones: clean regularly with chlorine solution; replace when bubble flow is inconsistent (no interval) — same source `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Hiblow linear diaphragm pumps (manufacturer maintenance guide): HP-10/20 "Replace the diaphragms and the valves with new ones regularly (every one year or one and half years)"; HP-30/40/50, HP-60/80 and HP-100/120 "at least once a year or one and a half years"; "Parts require repair or maintenance at least once a year"; filter: "If the filter is dirty, dust it off well" (no interval); one-year warranty; "seven-year guarantee on all spare parts even if the pump go out of production"; failure signs "loud, irregular noise" or "no air comes from reservoir" with "Diaphragm damaged" as a cause; HP-60/80 and HP-100/120 have a safety device: "If the pump stops automatically, the safety screw must be broken to prevent any further damage" — [Hiblow HP-series maintenance guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/2394/4493/files/Hiblow_HP-Series_Maintenance_Guide_PDF.pdf) `[tier_2 (manufacturer) | confidence: high, maker's own service interval | single_source]`

- Warranty, Crompton OWE052: 1 year — [Crompton](https://www.crompton.co.in/products/owe0521phz-21fs) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Secoh operating envelope: ambient -10 °C to 40 °C, RH ≤ 90 %, install above water level; "less maintenance" claimed without an interval — [BIBUS Secoh documentation](https://www.bibus.nl/media/wysiwyg/Pompen/Secoh-lucht-pompen/BIBUS_SECOH_Documentation_EN-10-25.pdf) `[tier_2 | confidence: high | single_source]`

## Q6. What growers use instead, and what fails (failure modes, time to fish/plant loss)

### Takeaway
Tier_1 sources agree that losing aeration or circulation is fast and severe. FAO: fish "can suffer and die within a few hours" if flow stops. SRAC 453: restore power or blower service in "less than 20 minutes"; a short interruption "can lead to complete loss of fish". Plant-side tolerance depends on grow method: rafts buffer, gravel beds and NFT wilt quickly. Documented alternatives are airlifts, intermittent operation, AC/DC or battery air pumps, generators, liquid/compressed oxygen and duplicated pumps. No measured hours-to-loss figure for aquaponics was found.

### Cited Findings
- Fish: if water flow stops, "fish can suffer and die within a few hours" — [FAO 589 sec. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium, qualitative | corroborated in direction by SRAC 453]`
- Fish: "fish may die within hours when exposed to low DO within the fish tanks"; DO 3.0–5.0 ppm described as a "12-24 hour range of tolerance / stressful conditions" — [FAO 589 sec. 3.2.1 via SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/un-aquaponics-manual/46368284) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- RAS: power or blower service must be restored within "less than 20 minutes"; "Even a short term interference with aeration capabilities can lead to complete loss of fish" — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | corroborated in direction by FAO 589]`. Context: intensive RAS, higher density than small aquaponics; this is a context_split, not a single number.
- Plants by grow method: raft (DWC) systems are not immediately affected by a pumping disruption because the rafts hold water; in gravel beds "a disruption in flow will lead to the rapid onset of water stress (wilting)"; in NFT "a disruption in water flow can lead quickly to wilting and death" — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | single_source]`
- Case report: in the authors' power outage the fish came through 20 minutes without electricity with DO "actually higher than normal" because the cylinder-oxygen backup opened; they note outage-stressed fish may develop stress-induced disease "after several weeks". No DO decay rate or time-to-death was given — [Ebeling and Hightower 2010, Global Seafood Alliance](https://www.globalseafood.org/advocate/backup-oxygen-power-systems-essential-insurance/) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium, single incident | single_source]`
- DO behaviour: with the pump off, DO "will decrease constantly" and "increase rapidly" on restart; the thesis says experiments on "how long the living organisms can be without system power" are still needed — [Olafs 2014](https://core.ac.uk/download/217154634.pdf) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Low-density FAO DWC units without a water pump "are utterly dependent on adequate aeration" — [FAO 589 DWC](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/deep-water-culture-technique/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
- Alternatives documented:
  - Airlifts in place of water pumps at low lift (FAO 30–40 cm; SRAC < 46 cm for large airlifts); 51% energy reduction in one raft test — [FAO 589](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/), [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf), [Olafs 2014](https://core.ac.uk/download/217154634.pdf) `[tier_1/tier_2 | confidence: medium | corroborated on the low-lift condition]`
  - Intermittent (15 min on/off) circulation and aeration: 50% less air-pump energy, a stated 75% combined circulation saving and 44.1% more growth per kWh in one thesis system — [Olafs 2014](https://core.ac.uk/download/217154634.pdf) `[tier_2 | confidence: low, one small system, not replicated | single_source]`
  - AC/DC combination air pump for outages — [FAO 589](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
  - Cylinder oxygen on a power-fail solenoid (about $250 in 2010, US), plus a pressure switch for blower failure — [Ebeling and Hightower 2010](https://www.globalseafood.org/advocate/backup-oxygen-power-systems-essential-insurance/) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | single_source]`
  - Generator, liquid oxygen tanks or mechanical blower, plus alarms with auto-dialers — [SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: high | corroborated by Aqu@teach]`
  - Two pumps in parallel plus a spare, with automatic generator switch-over — [Aqu@teach/FarmHub](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/aquateach/12-design-and-build/connections-water-movement-and-aeration/) `[tier_2 | confidence: medium | conflicting with SRAC 454's single-pump advice; both recorded]`
  - UVI: pure oxygen generally not needed at its ~0.5 lb/gal density — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) `[tier_1 | confidence: medium | single_source]`

### Inferences
- Grow method changes which failure matters first. A DWC raft buffers the plants and leaves the fish as the time-critical part. NFT and gravel/media beds put plants at risk within the same outage. This is a context tag for the database (grow_method → failure_sensitivity), not a measured duration.
- "Within a few hours" (FAO, small units) and "less than 20 minutes" (SRAC, intensive RAS) are context-split values for different densities and system types. Do not merge them.
