# Aquaponics and hydroponics equipment inventory: fish tanks and sumps, grow units, plumbing

Scope: an evidence-first equipment inventory for a component database. It is not a farm design and does not size any farm. All prices were read on 2026-11-25 unless stated. No currency has been converted. Unit conversions (ft to m, gal to L) and simple divisions (pack price / pack area) are marked "derived" and use only the cited numbers. Nothing here is a claim of profitability, viability, fish safety or aeration adequacy.

Tag notation used on each value: `[tier | confidence: reason | corroboration]`.
- tier: T1 = peer-reviewed, government cost norms, university research station reports (UVI, SRAC); T2 = extension guides, research-institute notes, industry reports, manufacturer datasheets, government-funded company project reports; T3 = retail/B2B listings, forums, blogs, videos.
- corroboration: corr = 2+ independent sources agree; single = single_source; conflict = conflicting.
- Retail price confidence is "medium" at best: a listing is a single reading of an offer that can change, and VAT status, shipping and pack contents vary.

## Q1. What are the common options and their typical price ranges per market for each item?

### Takeaway
Only a partial price picture could be built. The best-documented item-level cost set is the UVI commercial raft system costed at 2014 US prices by Heidemann and Bailey (fish tanks USD 2,155 each, raceway walls USD 1,148 each, LDPE liner USD 2,170 total, 72 EPS rafts at USD 35.59 each, PVC parts USD 1,327). - SRAC 5007: media beds commonly "4 ft x 8 ft lined wooden beds, IBCs, or barrels"; depth "usually 6 to 12 inches"; keep water "no more than 1 inch below the top of the media to limit algae"; media "River rock (limestone-free), expanded clay pebbles, plastic biomedia, vermiculite, or perlite (less used now)" — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | high | single].

## Q3. What sizing rules do UVI, FAO 589, SRAC sheets, Nelson and Pade and extension guides give?

### Takeaway
The feed-rate ratio is the main published sizing rule: 60-100 g feed per m2 of raft per day (UVI via SRAC 454), restated as 56-100 g/m2/day in SRAC 5007, with gravel and NFT at about 25 % of the raft ratio (SRAC 454) or 25-75 % (SRAC 5007). Volume rules include 1 ft3 fish tank : 2 ft3 pea gravel (SRAC 454), clarifier and biofilter each 15-20 % of fish tank volume (SRAC 5007; FAO 589 says one-sixth each), sump one-fourth to one-third of fish tank volume (FAO 589) or at least 70 % of bed volume for flood-and-drain beds (FAO 589). FAO 589 (read via a web mirror) adds geometry rules: DWC canals 30 cm deep with 1-4 h retention; media beds 30 cm (fruiting) or 15-20 cm (leafy), 8-20 mm media; NFT pipes 7.5 cm (leafy) or 11 cm (fruiting), 1-12 m long, 1 cm/m slope, 1-2 L/min, 21 cm hole spacing; a 7.5 cm bell on a 2.5 cm standpipe for 1-3 m2 beds. Nelson and Pade rules were not found.

## Q4. What lifetimes are reported, and from what basis?

### Takeaway
The only lifetimes found are depreciation lives from one university economics study of the UVI system (Heidemann & Bailey, 2014 prices): 20 years for fish tanks, clarifiers, filter tanks, sump; 10 years for raceway walls, LDPE liner, EPS rafts, PVC parts, blowers; 5 years for the circulating pump. FAO 589 gives a guidance (not measured) pump life of 3-5 years and calls coconut-fibre media short-lived. No manufacturer lifetime claims were found on the Dutch IBC or pond-liner listings that were read.

### Cited Findings
- Depreciation lives in the UVI costing (Table 7): fish rearing tanks 20 yr; clarifiers/swirl separators 20 yr; net filter tanks 20 yr; degassing tank 20 yr; base addition tank 20 yr; sump 20 yr; hydroponic troughs (raceway walls) 10 yr; liner (LDPE) 10 yr; raft boards 10 yr; PVC parts 10 yr; other parts (PVC pipe, netting, airstones) 10 yr; ½ hp circulating pump 5 yr; blowers 10 yr; unspecified equipment 10 yr — [Heidemann & Bailey](https://sare.org/wp-content/uploads/Economic-Analysis-of-UVI-Commercial-Aquaponics-System.pdf) [T1 | medium: depreciation assumptions in one study, not measured service life | single]. ## Q5. What do growers use instead, and what fails?

### Takeaway
Common substitutes documented in T1 sources are IBCs, barrels and lined wooden beds (SRAC 5007), closed-cell polystyrene insulation board as raft (SRAC 5007, UVI EPS rafts) and LDPE film as trough liner (UVI). Documented failure points are clogging (media, microtubes, NFT, large-rooted crops in NFT pipes), pump or power loss (NFT dries out; fish can die within hours without water movement, per FAO 589), fish-tank drainage through human error ("a major cause of fish kills", FAO 589), limestone media raising pH, unwashed volcanic gravel fines harming gills, algae from exposed water or light-transmitting pipe, herbivorous fish eating DWC roots, and weak or water-absorbing homemade EPS rafts; XPS boards carry mandatory fire retardants that one raft developer judged unsuitable. FAO 589 also accepts IBCs, bathtubs, vinyl fence posts and lined in-ground ponds as substitutes.

### Cited Findings
- Substitutes: fish tanks from "UV-protected plastic, fiberglass, metal or wood with liner, intermediate bulk containers (IBCs), or barrels"; media beds from "4 ft x 8 ft lined wooden beds, IBCs, or barrels"; tank covers from shade cloth, foam board or netting — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | high | single].
- Raft substitutes: "Closed-cell polystyrene insulation board. Regular polystyrene is not suitable." — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | high | single]. XPS "due to the mandatory fire retardants, are not suitable for planned aquaponic raft manufacture"; homemade EPS rafts broke at 5.9 kg and absorbed the most water; LLDPE rafts absorbed no water but were heavier and more costly — [USDA NIFA CRIS 1012572 (Hawaii Fish Company)](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html) [T2 | medium | single]. A Cornell University aquaculture blog post is titled "Dow blue boards: no, no, no" (not read; position on XPS blue board inferred from title only, anecdote) — [cornelluniversityaquaculture.blogspot.com](https://cornelluniversityaquaculture.blogspot.com/2013/07/dow-blue-boards-no-no-no.html) [T3 | low | single]. Friendly Aquaponics "Raft Discussion" (forum/blog, not read) — [old.friendlyaquaponics.com](https://old.friendlyaquaponics.com/2017/03/08/raft-discussion) [T3 | low: anecdote | single].
- Failure modes and effects on fish and plants:
  - Clogging: unmanaged media or solids "can clog media and create anaerobic zones" — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | high | single]; microtubes "not recommended because they clog" — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) [T1 | high | single].
  - NFT pump/power failure: NFT is vulnerable to pump failure "from clogging or power loss"; roots limit long crops — [Maucieri et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-15943-6_4.pdf) [T1 | high | single]. Raft systems are described in the project's own corroboration report as resilient to short pump disruptions because plants remain in water (internal secondary source, not re-checked against primary in this session).
  - Media chemistry: limestone in media raises pH; nitrification lowers pH; expanded clay with pH above 7.0 is unsuitable — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf), [Maucieri et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-15943-6_4.pdf) [T1 | high | single each; the two sources make different points (limestone raises pH; high-pH clay unsuitable), so they are not counted as corroboration].
  - Algae: water standing above media or uncovered beds encourages algae — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | high | single].
  - Metals in plumbing: pipes and valves should be PVC/PE "without Ni or Cu parts" — [Maucieri et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-15943-6_4.pdf) [T1 | high | single].
  - Used containers: SRAC 5007 requires food-grade, thoroughly washed containers — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf); Dutch sellers offer IBCs "ongereinigd" (unwashed) at the lowest price — [binqer.nl](https://www.binqer.nl/opbergboxen/ibc-containers/ibc-container-1000-liter) [T1 + T3 | medium | single each].
  - Raft hygiene: bacteria present on all raft test discs before sanitation; chlorine rinse and 48 h drying effective — [USDA NIFA CRIS 1012572](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html) [T2 | medium | single].
  - Water loss: systems lose about 1-5 % of water daily — [SRAC 5007](https://srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5007%20Principles%20of%20Small-Scale%20Aquaponics.pdf) [T1 | medium | single].
  - Stone wool compaction and organic substrate decomposition cause aeration problems over cycles; oxidisers can build biofilm that clogs drippers — [Maucieri et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-15943-6_4.pdf) [T1 | high | single].
- UVI one-pump/one-low-point rule: the sump "should be the only tank where water level drops" — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf) [T1 | high | corr with FAO 589 CHIFT-PIST layout, where leaks cannot drain the fish tank].
- FAO 589 failure modes with fish/plant effects (via FarmHub mirror, [T1 | medium-high]):
  - Fish tank drainage is "a major cause of fish kills", usually from human error; without water movement fish can die within hours, media beds and DWC stagnate and NFT pipes dry out; unfiltered solids can produce hydrogen sulphide, "lethal to fish"; submersible pumps fail if run dry — [s.4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/).
  - Plumbing should be "black or opaque pipe to block light and prevent algae"; NFT grow pipes should be white because they "reflect sunlight and keep the interior cool" — [s.4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/), [s.4.4](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/nutrient-film-technique-nft/). (Pipe colour guidance differs by role: opaque/black for plumbing, white for NFT grow pipes; context_split.)
  - Large-rooted crops (tomato, mint) clog NFT pipes, causing overflows and water loss — [s.4.4](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/nutrient-film-technique-nft/).
  - DWC: herbivorous fish (tilapia, carp) in canals eat roots; letting rafts dry after harvest kills nitrifying bacteria — [s.4.5](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/deep-water-culture-technique/).
  - Media beds: volcanic gravel fines "can clog the system and harm fish gills" if not washed; bell siphons are "finicky" because timing depends on constant inflow — [s.4.3](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/the-media-bed-technique/).
  - Substitutes FAO accepts: modified IBCs and bathtubs as media beds; IBC or fibreglass DWC canals; vinyl fence posts and 10 x 7 cm rectangular profiles as NFT pipes; cement- or plastic-lined in-ground ponds as fish tanks; plastic bottle caps as (low surface area, 50-100 m2/m3) media — [s.4.2-4.5 via FarmHub](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/the-media-bed-technique/).
