# Aquaponics pH management practice, base dosing, alkalinity data and target-pH trade-offs

Scope: freshwater aquaponics with tilapia and leafy/herb crops (raft/DWC, NFT, media beds). This is evidence only, not canon. Tier labels follow the brief: tier_1 = peer-reviewed or official, tier_2 = extension or reputable grey literature, tier_3 = commercial or anecdotal. Any per-kg-feed or per-m3 figure that is not in a source is labelled INFERENCE and is not a reported value.

## Q1. How much base do operating aquaponics systems add (per kg feed, per day, per m3)?

### Takeaway
The only operating system with reported base masses is UVI (St. Croix): about 40.65 kg Ca(OH)2 plus 38.85 kg KOH per year (projected from the basil trials), in a 111.2 m3 system with rainwater make-up, holding pH 7.0–7.5. SRAC 454 gives the same order of magnitude: 500–1,000 g doses alternated several times a week. Turned into per-feed terms (inference), this is roughly 11–14 g of base per kg of feed. That is far below simple nitrification stoichiometry, which points to large alkalinity recovery from denitrification in the UVI solids and filter compartments, or to incomplete records. Treat it as a single-system, context-bound observation.

### Cited Findings
- **UVI base masses (basil trials).** Ca(OH)2: 22.5 kg in 23 doses over the trial and inter-trial period, projected to 40.65 kg/yr. KOH: 21.5 kg in 22 doses, projected to 38.85 kg/yr. The two bases are added alternately to a 189 L base-addition tank and enter the system slowly. pH is checked daily and held at 7.0–7.5. Source: Rakocy, J.E., Bailey, D.S., Shultz, R.C. & Thoman, E.S., "Update on tilapia and vegetable production in the UVI aquaponic system", Proc. 6th International Symposium on Tilapia in Aquaculture (ISTA6), c. 2004. The PDF carries no explicit year; ISTA6 was held in Manila in 2004. — [Rakocy et al., ISTA6](https://ag.arizona.edu:443/azaqua/ista/ista6/ista6web/pdf/676.pdf). Tier_1/2 (symposium proceedings from the system operator). Confidence: medium. The values are measured dose totals, but the annual figure is a projection and the trial duration is not restated. Corroboration: same order of magnitude as SRAC 454 (next item).
- **SRAC 454 dosing description.** At UVI, KOH and Ca(OH)2 are added in equal amounts, "usually 500 to 1,000 g", alternated several times a week to keep pH near 7.0. A separate base tank prevents pH spikes in the rearing tanks, and soluble KOH can create toxic high pH in the sump until it is diluted. — [Rakocy, Masser & Losordo, SRAC Publication No. 454 (2006)](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf). Tier_1 (official USDA-SRAC extension). Confidence: medium. The ranges are descriptive and the dose frequency is vague ("several times a week"). Corroborates the ISTA6 masses: 3 doses/wk × 0.5–1 kg ≈ 78–156 kg/yr against about 79.5 kg/yr in ISTA6 (INFERENCE arithmetic).
- **UVI context for normalizing (ISTA6).**
  - System and water: total volume 111,196 L; four 7.8 m3 rearing tanks; 214 m2 plant growing area; flow 378 L/min. Make-up water was rainwater, averaging 2.61 m3/day (2.4% of volume per day) in the basil trials.
  - Fish and feed: 32% protein floating feed. Feeding ratio 81.4 g/m2/day for batch basil and 99.6 g/m2/day for staggered basil. Nile tilapia FCR 1.7, projected 4.16 t/yr. Red tilapia FCR 1.8, projected 4.78 t/yr.
  - Supplements: only Ca, K (both via the bases) and Fe (chelate at 2 mg/L every 3 weeks; 18.17 kg of 13% Fe chelate in 8 doses).
  - Source: [Rakocy et al., ISTA6](https://ag.arizona.edu:443/azaqua/ista/ista6/ista6web/pdf/676.pdf). Tier_1/2. Confidence: medium-high, because these are design and operating facts.
- **Other UVI descriptions.** The ISTA9 commercial-system paper gives a pH target of 7.0, equal alternating Ca(OH)2/KOH, a 0.2 m3 base tank, make-up water of about 1.5% of the 110 m3 volume per day, an optimum feeding ratio of 60–100 g/m2/day, and filter-cleaning frequency used to control nitrate via denitrification. It gives no dose masses. — [Rakocy, Bailey, Shultz & Danaher, "A commercial-scale aquaponic system developed at UVI" (ISTA9)](https://cales.arizona.edu/azaqua/ista/ISTA9/PDF's/RakocyAquaponicSystem.pdf). Tier_1/2. Confidence: medium. Note the conflict on make-up water: 1.5%/day here against 2.4%/day in ISTA6. These are different periods and different reporting.
- **Bailey & Ferrarezi (2017).** This is a crop-valuation paper ($/m2/week) for the UVI Commercial Aquaponic System. Its abstract and landing page give no pH, alkalinity or base data. — [Bailey & Ferrarezi 2017, Aquaculture Reports 7:77–82, doi:10.1016/j.aqrep.2017.06.002](https://doaj.org/article/03261e3040c34e2b926d8a00da9f52f0). Tier_1. It is not a source for base use.
- **Savidov 2005 (Alberta).** pH was held near 6.1–6.3, described as optimal for mineral uptake and not harmful to tilapia. pH was raised with Ca(OH)2 or KHCO3, alternated weekly, and lowered with H3PO4, under continuous computer pH control. The fish hatchery ran at pH 6.5–7.0. No dose masses or alkalinity were reported. — [Savidov, N. 2005, "Evaluation and development of aquaponics production and product market capabilities in Alberta, Phase II", Final Report, Crop Diversification Centre South, Brooks AB (dated 20 Dec 2005)](https://issuu.com/ponics/docs/110504140652-36fd13b4bc2849e4895c5eeeb258c678). Tier_2 (government research report, not peer-reviewed). Confidence: medium. Primary report, but read via an Issuu copy.
- **Small-system practice (Cornell).** Daily 1 M K2CO3 additions held the aquaponic treatment at pH 7.0, and no acid was needed. Alkalinity was about 40 mg/L as CaCO3 (titrated to pH 4.5; ±4 mg/L) in the aquaponic tubs, against about 20 (pH 5.8) and about 40 (pH 7.0) in the hydroponic tubs. The fish were koi (~7 kg/m3) in a 1,000 L tank. Volumes of base were not reported in the abstract-level extraction. — [Vandam, Anderson, De Villiers & Timmons 2017, Horticulturae 3(2):32, doi:10.3390/horticulturae3020032](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-7524/3/2/32). Tier_1. Confidence: medium.
- **Small-system practice (Cornell, lettuce).** The A7 treatment used 1 M K2CO3 only. K2CO3 "increased alkalinity" and reduced pH fluctuation, but no numeric alkalinity was reported (Section 2.5). — [Anderson, de Villiers & Timmons 2017, Horticulturae 3:43, doi:10.3390/horticulturae3030043](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.cornell.edu/dist/1/3676/files/2019/09/Growth-and-Tissue-Elemental-Composition-Response-of-Butterhead-Lettuce.pdf). Tier_1. Confidence: medium.
- **Extension base lists without amounts.** The Kentucky State manual lists Ca(OH)2, CaCO3, KOH and K2CO3, alternating Ca and K. It gives no numeric doses and says to add "in small doses" (pp. 25–28). — [Hager, Bright, Dusci & Tidwell 2021, KSU Aquaponics Production Manual](https://www.kysu.edu/documents/school-of-agriculture-communities-and-the-environment/aquaponics_handbook_2021_final_022421.pdf). Tier_2. NMSU says to use equal amounts of Ca(OH)2 and KOH, "determined by trial and error", when pH falls below 6.4, restoring it to about 7.0. — [Sallenave, NMSU Circular 680](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR680/). Tier_2. FAO 589 suggests carbonate buffers (eggshell, shell, limestone grit) at "2–3 handfuls per 1,000 litres" in a porous bag in the sump. — [Somerville et al. 2014, FAO Fisheries and Aquaculture Technical Paper 589, Ch. 3 (FarmHub mirror)](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/3-water-quality-in-aquaponics/manipulating-ph/). Tier_1 (FAO official), mirrored text. Confidence: low as a quantitative value, since "handfuls" is not a measured dose.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE, UVI base per kg feed.** Total base = 40.65 + 38.85 = 79.5 kg/yr ≈ 0.218 kg/day.
  - Feed basis A: 81.4 g/m2/day × 214 m2 ≈ 17.4 kg feed/day, which gives about 12.5 g base per kg feed.
  - Feed basis B: 4.16 t/yr Nile tilapia × FCR 1.7 ≈ 7.07 t feed/yr, which gives about 11.2 g base per kg feed.
  - In CaCO3 equivalents (Ca(OH)2 × 1.351; KOH × 0.892): about 89.6 kg CaCO3-eq/yr, or roughly 13–14 g CaCO3-eq per kg feed.
  - Per volume: about 2.0 g base per m3 of system per day (0.218 kg/day ÷ 111.2 m3).
  - These are derived numbers, not reported ones. The feed basis is uncertain because the trial feed totals are not given.
- **INFERENCE, gap against stoichiometry.** The commonly used figure is about 7.14 g CaCO3 alkalinity consumed per g of TAN-N nitrified; it is not sourced in the pages fetched here, so verify it before using it. With a TAN production estimate of about 0.03 kg TAN/kg feed at 32% protein, full nitrification would need on the order of 0.2 kg CaCO3/kg feed, about 15 times the UVI implied dose. Plausible reasons:
  - Denitrification in clarifiers and filter tanks. UVI explicitly manages nitrate this way, and denitrification returns about 3.5 mg/L alkalinity per mg/L NO3-N reduced (Hawaii, Q2).
  - Plant nitrate uptake.
  - Incomplete dose records.
  - This is a key uncertainty for any model. Do not assume stoichiometric base demand in a UVI-type system.
- Base choice in practice is Ca(OH)2/KOH alternation (UVI, NMSU, Texas), K2CO3 (Cornell), or Ca(OH)2/KHCO3 (Savidov). Choosing Ca against K is also a nutrient decision: FAO 589 says Ca suits leafy crops (tip-burn) and K suits fruiting crops.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found giving base use per kg feed directly. UVI per-feed values above are derived.
- No dose masses found for Savidov, Knaus & Palm, or the Cornell systems.
- Bailey & Ferrarezi 2017 and Ferrarezi & Bailey 2019 (basil, HortTechnology 29(1):85–93) abstracts contain no pH, alkalinity or base data. The full texts were not checked.
- Rakocy et al. 2004 Acta Horticulturae 648 was not fetched. It may contain the same Ca(OH)2/KOH data.

## Q2. How fast do pH and alkalinity fall without dosing? Does plant nitrate uptake offset acid production?

### Takeaway
Every source agrees that nitrification acidifies the water and that UVI-scale systems need daily pH checks and dosing several times a week. No source found here reports a measured, quantitative pH or alkalinity decline rate in an unbuffered aquaponic system. The only "trajectory" data are a 14-day KSU table showing almost flat pH (6.54 to 6.48) and a non-quantitative "7 to 5 over two weeks" example. Denitrification returns alkalinity, which may explain low base needs. No measured data on alkalinity return from plant nitrate uptake were found.

### Cited Findings
- **UVI.** "Nitrification produces acid, so pH drops daily." pH is checked daily. Measured pH was 7.4 (7.0–7.6) in batch basil, 7.1 (6.9–7.3) in staggered basil, and 7.1 (6.8–7.4) in okra filter effluent. Alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) was 113.2 (65.6–206.7) batch basil, 88.6 (65.6–115.6) staggered basil, and 115.9 (96.7–155.6) okra. — [Rakocy et al., ISTA6](https://ag.arizona.edu:443/azaqua/ista/ista6/ista6web/pdf/676.pdf). Tier_1/2. Confidence: medium-high, as measured means with ranges. This is the best "observed alkalinity under dosing" dataset found.
- **KSU Table 9 (p. 30).** Alkalinity went from 16.25 to 18.21 (unit printed as "mg") over 14 days, bicarbonate from 19.81 to 22.21 ppm, and pH from 6.54 to 6.48. The manual's p. 25 example of a pH drop "from 7 to 5 over two weeks" is illustrative, not measured. — [Hager et al. 2021, KSU manual](https://www.kysu.edu/documents/school-of-agriculture-communities-and-the-environment/aquaponics_handbook_2021_final_022421.pdf). Tier_2. Confidence: low. The system context and dosing state are unclear, and the units look wrong.
- **Hawaii (Khanal lab).** Denitrification recovers about 3.5 mg/L alkalinity per 1 mg/L nitrate reduced to N2. Nitrification's first step produces acid and lowers pH. — [Univ. of Hawaii, Khanal aquaponics nitrogen page](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~khanal/aquaponics/nitrogen.html). Tier_2 (university lab web page). Confidence: medium, since this is standard stoichiometry. Corroborated in spirit by Masabni & Sink: a pH that does not decline can indicate denitrification, which raises pH. — [Masabni & Sink, Texas A&M EHT-147 (c. 2020)](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf). Tier_2.
- **FAO 589.** Nitrification and respiration naturally acidify water. pH can instead rise with high-KH source water, high evaporation plus carbonate-rich top-up, too few fish, or (in rainfed systems) not at all, so bases are needed. — [Somerville et al. 2014, FAO FTP 589 Ch. 3 (mirror)](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/3-water-quality-in-aquaponics/manipulating-ph/). Tier_1. Confidence: medium (qualitative).
- **Cornell.** In a koi aquaponic system at pH 7.0, nitrification and respiration "kept pH stable", so only K2CO3 base was needed and no acid. — [Vandam et al. 2017](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-7524/3/2/32). Tier_1. Confidence: medium (qualitative).

### Inferences
- In UVI-type systems with clarifiers and filter-tank denitrification and rainwater make-up, net alkalinity loss appears much smaller than stoichiometric nitrification alone predicts (see the Q1 inference). Water exchange (2.4%/day rainwater) dilutes but adds no alkalinity.
- Media beds with anoxic zones may behave similarly. Zou et al. 2016 attribute 75–79% of N2O to denitrification in media-based systems (Q3), so denitrification is active there.

### Gaps
- No measured pH or alkalinity decline rate (e.g., mg CaCO3/L/day, or pH units/day) found for an unbuffered aquaponic system. Tyson et al. 2004/2008, Zou 2016 and Wongkiew 2017 were not found to report this in the accessible text.
- No measured partition of alkalinity return from plant NO3- uptake (OH-/HCO3- efflux) in aquaponics was found. The question is open.
- The Wongkiew et al. 2017 review (Aquacultural Engineering 76:9–19) and Wongkiew et al. 2018 (Bioresour. Technol.) full texts were not accessed. Derikvand et al. 2024 cite Wongkiew 2018 as finding lower nitrate and higher ammonium at pH 6 than at pH 7 ([Derikvand et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10867649/)), which is secondary.

## Q3. Target pH trade-offs: fish, nitrifiers, plant nutrients, NH3, and crop yield

### Takeaway
Most extension and official guidance converges on a compromise near pH 7.0, with a range of 6.8–7.0 and a tolerable band of about 6.4–7.4. That rests on three points: nitrification is faster at higher pH, Fe/Mn/P availability falls above 7, and tilapia tolerate 5–10. The peer-reviewed data conflict on the best value:
- Tyson et al. 2008 (cucumber, perlite) recommended pH 8.0 for TAN removal.
- Zou et al. 2016 (media-based) recommended pH 6.0 for N-use efficiency.
- Savidov ran a commercial-scale system at 6.1–6.3.
- Derikvand 2024 found lab systems at pH 5.8–6.0 nitrified adequately, with comammox dominant.

For leafy crops at pH 7 in aquaponics, Cornell found lettuce at 95% of the pH-5.8 hydroponic control, while hydroponic lettuce at pH 7 fell to 77%.

### Cited Findings
**Nitrification against pH**
- **Tyson et al. 2004 (Florida).** Nitrification rate was tested in water at target pH 5.5/6.5/7.5/8.5, about 28–30 °C, from 5 mg/L TAN. TAN reached 0 in 12 days at pH 8.5, 20 days at pH 7.5, and 20–24 days at pH 6.5. At pH 5.5 there was no nitrification. Peak nitrite was 5.3 and 4.5 mg/L at day 12 at pH 8.5. Bases were NaHCO3 + KOH (Exp. 1) and KOH only (Exp. 2); acid was H3PO4. Alkalinity was kept above 50 mg/L (Exp. 1) and above 100 mg/L (Exp. 2). The authors suggested aquaponic pH of 6.5–7.0. — [Tyson, Simonne, White & Lamb 2004, Proc. Fla. State Hort. Soc. 117:79–83](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/download/85855/82771/107719). Tier_1 (proceedings). Confidence: medium. Small vessel trials with no plants or fish. Note the text conflict on first nitrite at pH 6.5 (days 16–24 in the Results, 20–24 in the Abstract).
- **Tyson et al. 2008 (perlite biofilters with cucumber).** TAN removal was 19, 31 and 80 g/m3/day at pH 6.0, 7.0 and 8.0. Early marketable cucumber yield fell linearly from 1.5 to 0.7 kg/plant from pH 6 to 8, but total marketable yield did not differ. Recommended pH was 8.0, except 7.0 for early-season cucumber. — [Tyson, Simonne, Treadwell, White & Simonne 2008, HortScience 43(3):719–724](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfb69e63b8185d9cae3f3a). Tier_1. Confidence: medium-high for the TAN-removal numbers. This is a fruiting crop, not a leafy one.
- **Zou et al. 2016 (media-based aquaponics).** At pH 6.0, 7.5 and 9.0, N-utilization efficiency was 50.9%, 47.3% and 44.7%. The N2O conversion ratio fell from 2.0% to 0.6% as pH rose from 6.0 to 9.0, and denitrification produced 75.2–78.5% of N2O. N compounds stayed tolerable at all pH levels. The authors suggested pH 6.0. — [Zou, Hu, Zhang, Xie, Guimbaud & Fang 2016, Bioresource Technology 210:81–87, doi:10.1016/j.biortech.2015.12.079 (Europe PMC record)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.biortech.2015.12.079&resultType=core&format=json). Tier_1. Confidence: medium. Abstract only; the fish and plant species and the base used were not verified here.
- **Derikvand et al. 2024 (lab aquaponics, lettuce, 23 °C, 3.5 months).** Systems ran at pH 7.6–7.8 and 5.8–6.0. The low-pH systems removed ammonium more slowly and stabilized later, but nitrified with comammox *Nitrospira* dominant. Lettuce biomass trended higher at pH 5.8–6.0 (p = 0.11, not significant). Fish biomass did not differ and no fish died. — [Derikvand, Sauter, Keddie & Stein 2024, iScience 27(3):109073, doi:10.1016/j.isci.2024.109073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10867649/). Tier_1. Confidence: medium (lab scale).
- **Extension optima.**
  - Nitrosomonas is best at about 7.8–8.0 and Nitrobacter at 7.3–7.5. Nitrosomonas is inhibited below 6.5 and activity stops at 6.0 or below. — [Hawaii Khanal page](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~khanal/aquaponics/nitrogen.html). Tier_2.
  - Nitrosomonas is best at about 7.0–8.0 and Nitrobacter at 7.5–8.0. Nitrification is inhibited at 6.0 and bacteria die below 5.5. — [Masabni & Sink EHT-147](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf). Tier_2.
  - Biofilters are best at 7.0–9.0, more efficient toward the high 8s. — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf). Tier_1.
  - Nitrification is best at 7.5 or above and nearly stops below 6.0. — [Rakocy, "Ten Guidelines for Aquaponic Systems" (Aquaponics Journal, grey literature)](https://cdn.aquaponicsassociation.org/uploads/2019/10/Aquaponics-Journal-10-Guidelines.pdf). Tier_2.
  - Nitrospira is best at 8.0–8.3, Nitrobacter at 7.5 and Nitrosomonas at 7.0–7.5 (citing Goddek et al. 2016). — [Yildiz et al. 2017, Water 9(1):13, doi:10.3390/w9010013](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/9/1/13). Tier_1 review, secondary.
  - Corroborated: all agree that the rate rises from 6 to 8+. The "stops at 6.0" claim conflicts with the Derikvand 2024 and Zou 2016 observations of functioning nitrification at pH 5.8–6.0, which suggests acclimated or comammox communities can nitrify at low pH.

**Plant nutrient availability against pH**
- Fe, Mn, Cu, Zn and B become less available above pH 7.0. P, Ca, Mg and Mo solubility drops sharply below 6.0. Hydroponic plants are best at 5.8–6.2 (acceptable 5.5–6.5). The compromise is close to 7.0. Too much Ca can precipitate P as dicalcium phosphate. Fe-DTPA stays soluble at pH 7, and Fe must be supplemented to 2 mg/L. — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf). Tier_1. Confidence: high (well-established).
- Above pH 7.5, plants "quickly become deficient" in Fe, P and Mn (p. 25). Fe-DTPA is most stable at 6.0–7.5 (p. 29). — [KSU manual 2021](https://www.kysu.edu/documents/school-of-agriculture-communities-and-the-environment/aquaponics_handbook_2021_final_022421.pdf). Tier_2.
- Above pH 7.0, micronutrient and P solubility falls (citing Tyson 2004), and P availability is lower at high pH through calcium phosphate formation (citing Cerozi & Fitzsimmons 2016). — [Yildiz et al. 2017](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/9/1/13). Tier_1 review.

**Leafy-crop yield at pH 5.8 against 7.0 (Cornell, raft/DWC tubs, koi RAS water, about 25 °C root zone)**
- Butterhead lettuce cv. Flandria, shoot fresh weight (Table 6):
  - H5 (hydroponic, pH 5.8): 178 g (100%).
  - H7 (hydroponic, pH 7.0): 137 g (77%).
  - A7 (aquaponic, pH 7.0): 169 g (95%; not different from H5).
- Shoot tissue (Table 10):
  - Mn: 77 (H5), 65 (H7) and 45 (A7) mg/kg; A7 was significantly lower than H5.
  - Ca: 1.37%, 1.33% and 1.22%; A7 was lower.
  - Fe (about 60 mg/kg) and P (1.03–1.11%) did not differ.
- Source: [Anderson et al. 2017, Horticulturae 3:43](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.cornell.edu/dist/1/3676/files/2019/09/Growth-and-Tissue-Elemental-Composition-Response-of-Butterhead-Lettuce.pdf). Tier_1. Confidence: medium. Single trial, koi not tilapia.
- Spinach cv. Carmel, 14-day grow-out: A7 (aquaponic pH 7.0) did not differ from H5 (pH 5.8) in fresh or dry weight. The slightly higher H7 weight was attributed to light position. — [Vandam et al. 2017, Horticulturae 3(2):32](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-7524/3/2/32). Tier_1. Confidence: medium. Corroborates the lettuce result that aquaponic water at pH 7 can match hydroponics at pH 5.8.
- Savidov kept pH at 6.1–6.3 as "optimal for mineral uptake" with tilapia. Basil reached 11.9 kg/m2 of greenhouse area in 2004/05. — [Savidov 2005](https://issuu.com/ponics/docs/110504140652-36fd13b4bc2849e4895c5eeeb258c678). Tier_2. UVI basil at pH 7.0–7.5 reached projected 25.0 (batch) and 23.4 (staggered) kg/m2/yr. — [Rakocy et al. ISTA6](https://ag.arizona.edu:443/azaqua/ista/ista6/ista6web/pdf/676.pdf). These yields are not comparable: they differ in climate, area basis (greenhouse area against growing area), density and cultivar, so they cannot isolate a pH effect.

**Fish (tilapia) tolerance and recommended system pH**
- Tilapia tolerate pH 5–10, and Nile tilapia grow best at 7–8. The recommended system pH is 7 ("a compromise"), with a table target of 6–7. — [Masabni & Sink EHT-147](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf). Tier_2. Confidence: medium.
- The compromise is 6.8–7.0, the tolerable range for fish, plants and bacteria is 6.4–7.4, the tilapia-system table gives 6.5–7, and tilapia tolerate 5.0–10.0. — [Sallenave, NMSU CR-680](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR680/). Tier_2. This corroborates Texas on tilapia tolerance.
- Fish tolerate 6.0–8.5 with slow acclimation, and the aquaponics compromise is 6–7 (Table 6, p. 24). — [KSU manual 2021](https://www.kysu.edu/documents/school-of-agriculture-communities-and-the-environment/aquaponics_handbook_2021_final_022421.pdf). Tier_2.
- The aquaculture recommendation is 6.5–8.5 (citing Timmons et al. 2002). — [Yildiz et al. 2017](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/9/1/13); also [Tyson et al. 2004](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/download/85855/82771/107719). Tier_1.

**NH3 interaction**
- Below pH 8, less than 10% of TAN is NH3, and the share rises sharply above that and with temperature. — [Hawaii Khanal page](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~khanal/aquaponics/nitrogen.html). Tier_2.
- Large single base doses can convert TAN to toxic NH3, so add base slowly. — [Masabni & Sink](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf); [NMSU CR-680](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR680/). Tier_2, corroborated.
- Conversely, low pH lets ammonia build up because nitrification slows. — [Rakocy Ten Guidelines](https://cdn.aquaponicsassociation.org/uploads/2019/10/Aquaponics-Journal-10-Guidelines.pdf). Tier_2.
- These are context statements, not fish-safety claims.

### Inferences
- The trade-off has three regimes.
  - pH about 7.0–7.5 (UVI) maximizes nitrification safety margin, at the cost of Fe/Mn availability (requiring Fe-DTPA) and slightly lower tissue Mn and Ca.
  - pH about 6.0–6.3 (Savidov, Zou) favours plant nutrient uptake and N-use efficiency, but brings slower and less robust nitrification and higher N2O ratios. It also needs acid (H3PO4) when source water is hard.
  - pH 8 (Tyson 2008) maximizes TAN removal, but raises NH3 fraction and micronutrient lock-up.
- A model should keep these as context-split options rather than one universal optimum.
- The leafy-crop yield penalty at pH 7 appears much smaller in aquaponic water (about 5% for lettuce, in one study) than in inorganic hydroponic solution (23%). A plausible explanation is organic chelation or microbial effects, but this is not proven.

### Gaps
- No basil yield comparison across pH 6, 7 and 8 in aquaponics or hydroponics was found in accessible sources.
- No tilapia-specific aquaponic growth comparison across pH was found. Tilapia ranges come from extension sources.
- The dependence of nitrification rate on pH in acclimated aquaponic biofilms (rather than start-up trials) is only partially quantified (Tyson 2008 rates).

## Q4. Alkalinity targets and the NaHCO3 rule

### Takeaway
Guidance is at least 100 mg/L as CaCO3 (SRAC 454, NMSU) or 50–150 (preferably above 100; Texas A&M), with KSU at 60–140. UVI's measured alkalinity (about 89–116 mg/L means) sits in this band. SRAC 454 and FAO 589 both say never use NaHCO3 in aquaponics because of sodium (keep Na below 50 mg/L). Tyson et al. 2004 nonetheless used NaHCO3 in a plant-free nitrification test.

### Cited Findings
- Biofilters perform best at total alkalinity of 100 mg/L or more (and BOD below 20 mg/L). Never use NaHCO3; keep Na in hydroponic solutions below 50 mg/L. Dolomite can be used to supply Mg. — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf). Tier_1. Confidence: medium-high.
- Acceptable alkalinity is 50–300 mg/L, recommended 50–150 (preferably above 100). Freshwater fish need 20 or more. — [Masabni & Sink EHT-147](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf). Tier_2. NMSU gives at least 100 mg/L. — [CR-680](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_circulars/CR680/). KSU gives 60–140 mg/L (p. 27). — [KSU 2021](https://www.kysu.edu/documents/school-of-agriculture-communities-and-the-environment/aquaponics_handbook_2021_final_022421.pdf). These are corroborated around 100 mg/L.
- Do not use sodium bicarbonate in aquaponics, because the sodium harms plants (it is used in RAS). Use CaCO3/K2CO3 (safer) or Ca(OH)2/KOH (strong; add slowly). If pH goes above 7 after adding carbonate material, remove it. FAO gives no numeric KH target. — [FAO FTP 589 (mirror)](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/3-water-quality-in-aquaponics/manipulating-ph/). Tier_1. This corroborates SRAC 454.
- Tyson et al. 2004 kept alkalinity above 50 and above 100 mg/L using NaHCO3, and specific conductance was higher in the NaHCO3 experiment (521 against 493 µS/cm). — [Tyson et al. 2004](https://journals.flvc.org/fshs/article/download/85855/82771/107719). This conflicts with the NaHCO3 rule, but the context was a plant-free vessel test.
- Observed values: UVI alkalinity 65.6–206.7 mg/L under Ca(OH)2/KOH dosing ([ISTA6](https://ag.arizona.edu:443/azaqua/ista/ista6/ista6web/pdf/676.pdf)). Cornell aquaponic tubs at about 40 mg/L at pH 7.0 with K2CO3 ([Vandam et al. 2017](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-7524/3/2/32)). Cornell worked well below the 100 mg/L guideline without reported nitrification failure.
- Too-high pH or alkalinity: above 7.5, Fe/P/Mn deficiencies set in quickly (KSU p. 25). Excess Ca precipitates P (SRAC 454). Hard, alkaline source water may need acid (H3PO4 preferred; avoid citric acid, which is antimicrobial) dosed into the top-up reservoir (FAO 589). Sources as above.

### Inferences
- About 100 mg/L as CaCO3 is a consensus extension target, but the supporting rationale (biofilter performance) is stated rather than demonstrated in aquaponics-specific data found here. Cornell's 40 mg/L systems suggest lower alkalinity is workable with frequent dosing.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed aquaponics study found that tests alkalinity levels against nitrification or crop outcome.
- No sourced upper alkalinity limit specific to aquaponics, other than Texas's "acceptable to 300 mg/L".

## Q5. Knaus & Palm and Savidov specifics; dissolved CO2

### Takeaway
Savidov held pH 6.1–6.3 by continuous computer control, raising it with Ca(OH)2 or KHCO3 (alternated weekly) and lowering it with H3PO4. Alkalinity and source-water chemistry were not reported. For Knaus & Palm, the accessible abstracts give no pH, alkalinity, hardness, base or exchange data, so the requested details remain a gap. For CO2, the only sourced value is a guideline of 20 ppm or less (Texas A&M). No measured dissolved CO2 in an aquaponic system was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Savidov 2005.** pH 6.1–6.3, Ca(OH)2/KHCO3 alternated weekly, H3PO4 for lowering, continuous computer monitoring, hatchery at 6.5–7.0, no alkalinity or source-water chemistry reported. Tilapia 100 g to 700 g in 24 weeks, expected FCR 1.3. Brooks yields: tomato 20.7 kg/plant/yr, mini-cucumber 33.4 kg/plant/yr, basil 11.9 kg/m2. — [Savidov 2005 final report](https://issuu.com/ponics/docs/110504140652-36fd13b4bc2849e4895c5eeeb258c678). Tier_2. Confidence: medium.
- **Knaus & Palm 2017a.** Two coupled ebb-and-flood gravel systems with no separate biofilter, Nile tilapia against African catfish, growing basil, parsley and marjoram. Feed was 70–450 g (max 150 g/day) over 70 days. Plant growth was about twice as high in the tilapia unit. The abstract reports no pH, alkalinity, hardness or base data. — [Knaus & Palm 2017, Aquaculture 466:51–63, doi:10.1016/j.aquaculture.2016.09.025](https://forschung.uni-giessen.de/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1127336611?lang=en_GB). Tier_1. The companion paper, "Effects of the fish species choice on vegetables in aquaponics under spring-summer conditions in northern Germany", Aquaculture (2017) pp. 62–73, also had no water-chemistry data on its metadata page. — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4ObjOAm7).
- **CO2 guidance.** Keep CO2 at 20 ppm or less. Diffused aeration vents CO2, and buildup is mainly a concern in pure-O2 systems. — [Masabni & Sink EHT-147](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2020/10/Water-Quality-In-Aquaponics-Sink-Masabni.pdf). Tier_2. Confidence: medium (guideline, not measurement).
- **UVI degassing.** UVI runs a continuously operating 0.7 m3 degassing tank (CO2, CH4, H2S, N2) with no CO2 targets. — [Rakocy et al. ISTA9](https://cales.arizona.edu/azaqua/ista/ISTA9/PDF's/RakocyAquaponicSystem.pdf). SRAC 454 notes that CO2 is released from culture water into enclosed greenhouses, and that low DO goes with high CO2, which promotes root pathogens. — [SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf).
- **Yildiz et al. 2017.** No aquaponic CO2 concentration is given. The review names hypercarbia as a RAS and aquaponics welfare risk and lists signs of toxicity. — [Yildiz et al. 2017](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/9/1/13).

### Inferences
- With strong aeration, as in raft systems, CO2 is usually stripped. This matters for pH: in a degassed system, measured pH reflects mainly alkalinity, not CO2 accumulation. This is a stated rationale from Masabni & Sink and UVI degassing, not a measurement.

### Gaps
- No measured dissolved CO2 (mg/L) in any aquaponic system was found within the fetch budget.
- Knaus & Palm pH (~8.0 per the brief), hardness, alkalinity, base addition, water exchange and source water were not verified. The full texts are paywalled and were not accessed.
- Savidov's source-water alkalinity and acid/base volumes are not reported.
- Not fetched: Rakocy et al. 2004 Acta Hort. 648, Goddek et al. 2015, Yep & Zheng 2019, and the Wongkiew 2017 full text.
