# Comparative summary  
- **CAPEX basis:** Media-bed systems scale mainly with the volume of inert media (essentially bed volume), whereas raft/DWC systems scale with raft surface area (and associated water volume), and NFT systems scale with total channel length (or number of channels).  Media beds typically use ~0.3–0.35 m bed depth【20†L307-L315】, so bed volume (area×depth) is a natural sizing metric.  Raft/DWC beds are wide (4–8 ft) and long (tens of feet)【28†L247-L255】, making surface area (and corresponding fish tank volume) the key driver.  NFT uses long (often 4–6 in diameter) channels【28†L293-L299】, so channel length (and thus number of plant sites) best correlates with scale.  
- **Maintenance tasks:** All systems share basic daily/weekly tasks (feed fish, check pumps and water flow, remove waste/uneaten feed, test water, inspect fish and plants【22†L745-L754】【30†L611-L619】).  **Media beds** uniquely require managing flood-drain cycles and periodic media agitation (flush the bed and clear root-blockages)【22†L775-L782】【30†L611-L619】.  **DWC/raft beds** require cleaning the raft tank and clarifier (removing solids accumulating under rafts) and ensuring adequate aeration of the floating beds.  **NFT channels** require frequent channel cleaning (flushing or soaking to prevent biofilm/algae clogs) and maintaining high flow to prevent root blockage (NFT pipes are upsized to avoid clogging【28†L293-L299】).  
- **Maintenance/labor drivers:** Many tasks are routine (e.g. daily pump and fish checks) and handled per time period, while others scale with system size.  Tasks like filter/clarifier cleaning scale with media volume or fish loading.  Plant-related tasks (harvest, seedling, pruning) scale with number of plant sites or bed area.  For example, fish feeding is per day (fixed), but filter cleaning effort will scale by bed volume (more media requires more cleaning).  In general, **media beds’** maintenance tends to scale with bed volume and plant area; **DWC/rafts** with raft area (or plant count); **NFT** with channel length (or plant count).

## Schema implications  
- **`capex_models.json`:** Introduce architecture-specific sizing fields.  E.g. define `media_bed_volume` (m³) for media-bed CAPEX, and use it as primary basis for media-bed grow unit costs.  For DWC, use `raft_area` (m²) as primary size param (with optional `fish_tank_volume` or `water_depth`).  For NFT, include `channel_length` (m) or `channel_count`.  These fields allow CAPEX scaling by the most relevant dimension.  Maintain secondary options (`grow_area`, `plant_count`) as alternate size keys with `schema_choice_todo` status.  
- **`cost_models.json`:** Record architecture-conditioned recurring costs.  For media beds, costs should include periodic bed flushing/cleaning tasks (labor and time).  For DWC/NFT, include costs for filter cleaning and channel maintenance.  Entry examples: `pump_maintenance_cost`, `filter_clean_cost`, each with driver basis (e.g. per cycle or per volume).  Many maintenance costs will be driven by feed rate or biomass (defer to simulation inputs for frequency).  
- **`labor_models.json`:** Catalog specific tasks as separate labor categories.  E.g. “inspect_and_clean_pumps” (per day), “remove_settled_solids” (per day or per volume), “media_bed_flush” (per event), “clean_raft_tank” (per event), “flush_NFT_channels” (per event), “planting/harvest” (per plant-site per crop cycle).  Each task definition should list its driver basis (day/cycle/area).  Architecture-intrinsic tasks (e.g. `clear_bed_roots`) get tagged for media beds only.  Tasks that scale with area or plant count (e.g. planting) should note alternative bases.  This library lets simulations compute labor by applying relevant driver rate to these tasks.  

## Better decided by simulation later  
- **Filter/Clarifier maintenance frequency:** The optimal interval for cleaning filters or removing sludge depends on actual feed loading and fish stocking; leave these for simulation to calculate rather than set a fixed schedule.  
- **Root and algae clearing:** Tasks like cleaning plant roots from channels or scrubbing algae off rafts are highly context-dependent (crop type, light, season); mark them as scenario-specific.  
- **Harvest labor:** Labor per harvest event should use actual plant-site count and crop-cycle length (dependent on crop species and management); defer to simulation that models cropping cycles.  
- **Water re-aeration and leveling:** How often water levels/pH need adjustment depends on transpiration rates and buffering; not fixed per architecture and best handled dynamically.  

## Open schema-choice TODOs  
- **Sizing basis for CAPEX:** Should media-bed CAPEX scale by volume (depth×area) or by footprint area?  Both can be useful: volume ties to filtration capacity; area ties to planting. This choice may remain a schema-level option.  
- **Multi-method systems:** Many commercial systems combine methods【57†L19-L27】. Deciding how to allocate CAPEX between media, raft and NFT components in hybrid layouts (e.g. a unit with both media beds and rafts) may need further design (likely a simulation or custom mapping).  
- **Labor drivers:** For tasks like filter cleaning or plant harvest, whether to use per-area, per-crop-cycle, or per-plant-site basis is undecided.  We may leave multiple driver fields (e.g. `labor_per_area` vs. `labor_per_site`) and let scenarios pick.  
- **Component variants:** The component library might need distinct variants for media-bed filters vs raft-bed tanks vs NFT channels.  A final decision on exactly which sizing fields to use as keys (e.g. `grow_area` vs `media_volume`) is still pending.
