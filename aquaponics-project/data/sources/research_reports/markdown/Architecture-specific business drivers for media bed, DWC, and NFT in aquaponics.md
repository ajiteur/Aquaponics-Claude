# Architecture-specific business drivers for media bed, DWC, and NFT in aquaponics

## Executive summary

The strongest evidence supports treating **media bed**, **deep water culture raft**, and **nutrient film technique** as three different business archetypes rather than one generic “plant subsystem” class. **DWC or raft** is the clearest commercial aquaponics archetype for leafy greens and herbs: extension sources describe it as the dominant leafy-green method in aquaponics, characterise it as less outage-sensitive than NFT, and tie it to fast harvest, standardised spacing, and head or live-root product forms. citeturn4view0turn8view1turn10view0turn33view0turn28search0turn11search0

**NFT** is the most channelised and the most failure-sensitive architecture. It is well suited to smaller, lighter-rooted crops, can support high plant density and efficient use of greenhouse space, and its channels can be longer-lived and easier to clean than foam rafts. But it also requires cleaner water, more efficient solids removal, often a separate biofilter, and uninterrupted flow; multiple high-trust sources state that clogging and flow interruption can quickly cause wilting or death. citeturn4view0turn10view0turn33view0turn31view2turn31view3turn29search1

**Media beds** are the most operationally flexible, because they support a wider crop mix and can collapse plant support, solids capture, and biofiltration into one component. That flexibility comes with meaningful business penalties: heavier structures, more difficult cleaning, root and solids clogging risk, more tedious crop turn on short-cycle greens, and limits on scale. The evidence therefore points to media beds as a better fit for mixed-crop, smaller-scale, or direct-market models than for highly standardised, high-throughput leafy-green lines. citeturn33view0turn24view0turn25view0turn32view0turn22search28

For schema design, the practical implication is simple: **architecture must be a first-class key** in capex, labour, yield-form, and downtime-risk models. Flattening media bed, DWC, and NFT into one business object would erase differences that the technical literature treats as operationally meaningful. citeturn4view0turn10view0turn33view0turn24view0

## Evidence posture

Your existing project evidence already captured two core operational truths that matter directly for the business layer: **NFT flow disruption can rapidly cause wilting or death**, and **reduced circulation in gravel or media systems can create anaerobic zones that impair or kill roots**. That prior corroboration should remain part of the canonical evidence chain for downtime-risk modelling. fileciteturn0file3

The public source stack used here extends those operational truths into business-driver structure. The main anchors are **SRAC 454** for system mechanics, filtration and biofiltration trade-offs, plant-architecture failure modes, and feed-to-plant sizing logic; **SRAC 5006** for economic framing, labour intensity, and architecture-by-scale fit; **SRAC 5009** for commercial crop-system choices, greenhouse-system cost anchors, harvest form, and post-harvest handling; **Oklahoma State HLA-6721** for media-bed versus raft workability and crop-method mapping; and **UVI crop-valuation work** for revenue cadence and harvest form in a DWC commercial archetype. Where aquaponic evidence was thinner, I used adjacent peer-reviewed hydroponic or decoupled-aquaponic studies only as **context splits**, not as universal rules. citeturn33view0turn4view0turn10view0turn24view0turn28search0turn17search0turn29search1turn16view0

The official FAO technical paper is easy to verify from FAO’s own metadata, including the fact that it covers all three main methods and includes a cost–benefit analysis of a small-scale media-bed unit. In this environment, the official PDF itself was not directly fetchable, so crop-method details were corroborated through Oklahoma State tables adapted from the FAO manual and through search snippets reflecting the FAO text. Those FAO-derived details are therefore best treated as **supporting context**, not as the sole basis for a canonical business rule. citeturn6view2turn6view3turn24view0turn22search28turn22search29

## Unresolved questions

- **Architecture-specific labour benchmarks are still thin.** High-trust sources clearly show that aquaponics is labour-intensive overall and that media beds versus rafts differ in harvest and cleaning effort, but I did not find strong commercial time-and-motion studies comparing labour hours per square metre or per harvest event across media bed, DWC, and NFT. That gap should remain a TODO in the business layer. citeturn4view0turn25view0turn32view0

- **Hard capex and COGS benchmarks are too context-sensitive to encode as universal constants.** SRAC gives a useful DWC versus NFT greenhouse cost example, but those numbers are not suitable as timeless canonical defaults. The category structure is strong; the exact amounts are not. citeturn10view0

- **Architecture-specific spoilage and post-harvest loss rates are under-documented.** The evidence strongly supports live-root or trimmed-root leafy handling for DWC and NFT, and it strongly supports frequent-delivery pressure for premium herb channels. It does not yet give reliable architecture-specific spoilage percentages. citeturn10view0turn4view0

- **Revenue-channel fit should remain partially inferred.** There is enough evidence to support architecture-specific output forms and operational fit, but not enough to hard-code universal price premiums by architecture. Keep the business layer structured for channel-specific pricing, but leave actual premiums as market evidence or operator-configured inputs. citeturn28search0turn4view0

- **System-stability economics need better data.** The technical literature clearly distinguishes outage sensitivity, solids sensitivity, and water-volume buffering across architectures, but the financial consequences of those differences have not been quantified consistently enough for a robust penalty curve. That should remain an evidence target for future internal logging. citeturn32view3turn31view2turn32view0
