# Equipment inventory: strong rules, thin prices

**Status: candidate data pending Ajit's review, not canon.** Every value below comes from the seven research notes in `research_notes/Aquaponics equipment inventory/` (all sources read 2026-11-25). Nothing here is written to `data/knowledge/**`. This is an inventory, not a farm design or sizing exercise. Nothing in it claims profitability, viability, fish safety, aeration adequacy, oxygen-transfer capacity or solids-removal capacity. Prices are listings, norms or study costs, not market ranges. No currency has been converted, because the notes cite no exchange rate and date. The machine-readable copy is `/home/user/reports/Aquaponics equipment inventory.jsonl` (169 lines, one per item and market).

The notes support **143 distinct items in 18 categories, stored as 169 item-and-market rows, and 110 of those rows carry a price**. The sizing rules are well sourced. They come from tier_1 manuals (UVI via SRAC 454, SRAC 453, SRAC 5007, FAO 589) and tier_2 extension guides. The prices are much weaker. Netherlands prices are mostly single-seller retail listings. Indian prices are government cost norms or IndiaMART "starting" prices with GST not stated. The US Virgin Islands figures come from one 2014 costing of the UVI system. Lifetimes are almost all depreciation assumptions, warranties or forum anecdotes, and no measured field life was found for any item. **The research was cut short by a shared web-fetch limit.** Many sources were located but never opened ("not reached"). That explains most of the empty cells, especially in heating and cooling, lighting, propagation, fish handling, post-harvest and Dutch greenhouse structures. It is not evidence that the items are absent from the market. The largest gaps are: Netherlands greenhouse, heating and LED prices; any Indian state price difference beyond seller location; dissolved-oxygen meter prices; dosing-chemical prices; and field lifetimes.

## Sizing rules are well sourced; prices and lifetimes are not

The strongest evidence is design guidance tied to feed or flow. For coupled raft systems, UVI's **60-100 g of feed per m² of raft per day** is the anchor ratio ([SRAC 454](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/SRAC-454pod.pdf)). FAO gives **40-50 g/m²/day for leafy greens and 50-80 g/m²/day for fruiting crops** at small scale ([FAO 589 ch. 8](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/8-management-and-troubleshooting/component-calculations-and-ratios/)). On the fish side, SRAC 453 sizes circulation at **42-84 L/min per kg of daily feed** ([SRAC 453](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/453%20Recirculating%20Aquaculture%20Tank%20Production%20Systems-%20A%20Review%20of%20Current%20Design%20Practice.pdf)). FAO instead uses fish-tank turnover: twice per hour for dense stocking and once per hour for low density, with a duty point of **2,000 L/h at 1.5 m** for small units ([FAO 589 s. 4.2](https://learn.farmhub.ag/resources/small-scale-aquaponic-food-production/4-design-of-aquaponic-units/essential-components-of-an-aquaponic-unit/)). The same pattern holds outside the water loop. Fan-and-pad cooling has extension rules: **8 cfm per ft² of floor** in one guide and one air change per minute in the other ([UMass](https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/greenhouse-floriculture/fact-sheets/fan-pad-evaporative-cooling-systems); [UF/IFAS AE069](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/AE069)). LED fixtures have a standards floor of **2.30 µmol/J** ([DLC Hort V3.0](https://designlights.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/DLC_HORT-Technical-Requirements-V3-0_FINAL_05132024.pdf)). CoolBot rooms have a published table matching room size to AC size ([E4C](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/solutions/product/coolbot-walk-cooler/)). These rules are recorded as sizing context in each row. None of them is applied to a farm here.

The only complete item-level cost set is the UVI commercial raft system, costed at 2014 prices in one university study. It lists rearing tanks at **USD 2,155 each**, raceway walls at USD 1,148.19 each, 72 EPS rafts at USD 35.59 each, a sump at USD 415 and a base addition tank at USD 110. It also gives depreciation lives: **20 years for tanks, 10 years for liner, rafts, PVC and blowers, and 5 years for the circulating pump** ([Heidemann & Bailey](https://sare.org/wp-content/uploads/Economic-Analysis-of-UVI-Commercial-Aquaponics-System.pdf)). Those lives are accounting assumptions, not measured service life. SRAC 5006 reports only whole-system investment taken from other studies, and its text (USD 1,020,536) disagrees with its own table (USD 1,030,536) for the largest system. It gives no item prices and no equipment lives, so it is cited only as context ([SRAC 5006](https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/5006%20Economics%20of%20Aquaponics.pdf)). The other lifetime figures each have a different basis, and they are kept separate rather than merged:

| Lifetime figure | Basis |
|---|---|
| FAO's 3-5 year submersible pump | Guidance statement |
| Hiblow's diaphragm replacement every 1-1.5 years | Maker interval |
| Victron LiFePO4: 2,500 cycles at 80% DoD | Cycle rating |
| DLC LED: Q90 ≥36,000 h | Hour standard |
| Bluelab pH probe: 6 months | Warranty |
| IBC totes: about 5 vs about 8 years outdoors | Two conflicting forum anecdotes |

## Each market prices a different kind of thing

**Netherlands and EU** prices are live retail listings read on one day, but almost every item comes from a single seller. That covers toppy.nl for pumps, air pumps, drum filter and UV-C; Hornbach and binqer.nl for IBCs and liners; and the Bluelab EU store for meters and controllers. None of these is a corroborated market range. VAT display also varies: binqer shows prices excluding BTW, while toppy, Hornbach and Bluelab show prices including it. The generator and engine-pump prices come from a **Honda Benelux list dated 1 January 2020**, so they are stale for 2026 ([Honda list](https://www.honda.nl/content/dam/local/be-ne-lux/industrial/pdf/Honda-Industrial-PRIJSLIJST-NL.pdf)). The firmest NL values are the 2026 water tariffs: **EUR 1.34/m³ incl. VAT (Vitens)** and **EUR 1.45 excl. / 1.58 incl. VAT (Dunea)**, plus a fixed charge and the tap-water tax ([Vitens](https://www.vitens.nl/-/media/Project/Vitens/VitensNl/Tarieven/Documenten-tarieven-2026/050004-31_Vitens-Tarieven-2026-Consument.pdf); [Dunea](https://www.dunea.nl/-/media/bestanden/over-dunea/tarievenregeling/dunea-tarievenregeling-2026.pdf)). No price was reached for a Dutch glasshouse, heat pump, boiler, LED fixture, DO meter or test kit.

**India** prices fall into two groups that should not be mixed: government norms and B2B listings. The norms are the firmest Indian numbers. They include NHB structure costs per m², for example **INR 844 for a tubular naturally ventilated polyhouse and INR 1,400 for fan-and-pad** ([NHB Scheme-1](https://nhb.gov.in/guideline/112.pdf)). They also include NFDB's RAS feed norm of INR 30/kg ([NFDB RAS](https://nfdb.gov.in/PDF/RAS%20RT.pdf)) and a Karnataka university's fingerling rates, for example **INR 4,000 per 1,000 tilapia fingerlings** ([KVAFSU FRIC Hebbal](https://www.kvafsu.edu.in/services_and_inputs/FRIC-Hebbal-Benagaluru-1.pdf)). The norms are undated or several years old.

IndiaMART listings make up most of the rest. Their GST status is not stated, and they are often "starting" prices for products that are poorly specified. State differences among Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana show up only as seller location, not as state price norms. Two findings stand out:

- **Coimbatore subsidy figures:** the district lists polyhouse at INR 467.50/m² and shade net at INR 355/m². These are exactly 50% of the national norms, so they are almost certainly subsidy amounts rather than structure costs, and they are not entered as prices ([Coimbatore Horticulture](https://coimbatore.nic.in/department-of-horticulture-plantation-crops/)).
- **Water tariffs:** Chennai charges **INR 99-163/kL** at commercial rates ([CMWSSB](https://www.cmwssb.tn.gov.in/tariff)). The Hyderabad figures are 2014 news reports. The Bengaluru figures could not be verified.

**Global and US reference** rows are used only where the target markets had nothing. They include UMD's 2023 build list, the 2010 backup-system costs of Ebeling and Hightower, University of Minnesota's 2024 CoolBot costs and the 2020 HPS cost per kW from Kusuma et al. They are reference points, not proxies for NL or Indian prices.

## Conflicts are kept side by side, not averaged

Where sources disagree, both values are stored, and the row is marked `conflicting` or treated as a context split. The disagreements fall into three groups.

Design and equipment conflicts:
- **UVI plant blower:** 1 hp in SRAC 454 and the UVI document, but 2 hp in the UVI costing.
- **LDPE liner cost:** USD 1,890 unit cost against a USD 2,170.08 total in the same costing table.
- **Oase pump curve at 1 m head:** 4,200 L/h against 4,800 L/h on one retailer page.
- **Bead-filter loading:** 13 kg feed/m³/day in the 1999 edition of SRAC 453 against 16-32 kg feed/m³/day in the 2013 edition.
- **NFT channel slope and length:** about 1% and up to 12 m (FAO 589) against about 0.42% and under 3.66 m (SRAC 5007).
- **Gravel and NFT feed ratio:** about 25% of the raft ratio (SRAC 454) against 25-75% (SRAC 5007).
- **Pump redundancy:** one circulation pump (SRAC 454) against two in parallel plus a spare (Aqu@teach).

Measurement and operating conflicts:
- **Meter calibration interval:** monthly (Bluelab) against yearly for two years (Atlas Scientific).
- **CoolBot minimum temperature:** 1 °C (Johnny's) against 0 °C (E4C).
- **Pad ratings:** 140 against 150 cfm/ft² (aspen), and 230 against 250 (cellulose).
- **Evaporative heat:** 8,700 against 8,100 Btu per gallon.

Price and practice conflicts:
- **Indian floating feed:** INR 30/kg (NFDB norm) against INR 47/kg for 30% protein feed (an IndiaMART listing).
- **Haryana NVPH cost:** INR 717.4-901/m² against INR 844-1,060/m² in the same state document.
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** an alkalinity buffer in RAS (SRAC 452), but "should not be used" for pH control in aquaponics (SRAC 454).
- **Rafts after harvest:** FAO says never let them dry, while a USDA NIFA raft project used a chlorine rinse plus 48 h of drying.
- **LED versus SON-T savings:** WUR found 10-25% total greenhouse energy saving, against the common claim of 40% less lamp electricity ([WUR](https://www.wur.nl/en/show/are-led-lamps-indeed-more-economical-than-son-t.htm)). The two figures use different system boundaries.

One tier decision also needs your review. **FAO 589 and SRAC 454 are recorded as tier_1, following the repo's Trusted Source Map, although two note files recorded them as tier_2.**

## What growers substitute, and what fails

Grower and extension sources agree on several substitutes:

- **IBC totes** replace purpose-built tanks. Their footprint is corroborated at 120 × 100 cm, and in NL they cost **EUR 59 (used, unwashed, excl. VAT) to EUR 369 (new, UV-stabilised, incl. VAT)**.
- **Insulation board** replaces purpose-made rafts.
- **PVC or square pipe** replaces NFT gullies.
- **Media beds** replace a separate biofilter.
- **Swirl separators** replace radial-flow settlers or drum filters.
- **Pond-filter kits** and aquarium air pumps replace specified pumps and blowers.

In India, **PVC-tarpaulin tanks (INR 4,100-10,000 per piece from one Guntur seller)** and bamboo or wooden polyhouses play the same role. Treated bamboo structures are quoted at **8-10 years** life by ICAR ([ICAR Indian Horticulture](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/download/148520/53882/472661)).

The evidence on how these substitutes fail is stronger than the evidence on how long they last:

- **Homemade EPS rafts** in a USDA-funded project gained more than 300% in weight from water uptake and broke at a 5.9 kg load ([USDA NIFA 1012572](https://portal.nifa.usda.gov/web/crisprojectpages/1012572-innovative-manufactured-aquaponic-raft-for-commercial-farms.html)).
- **Swirl separators** removed 37.1% of TSS against 77.9% for a radial-flow settler in one cold-water RAS trial. The authors still judged a full-flow drum filter "critical" ([Davidson & Summerfelt via GSA](https://www.globalseafood.org/advocate/settler-evaluation-radial-flow-vs-tangential-flow/)).
- **IBC residues** from previous contents are the dominant warning, raised by extension sources and by 7 of 8 posters in one forum thread.

The most-repeated failure in every category is **loss of power to water or air pumps**. FAO says fish "can suffer and die within a few hours" when flow stops. SRAC 453 asks for power or blower service to be restored in under 20 minutes. These statements are recorded as failure modes, not as fish-safety or aeration-adequacy claims.

Forum and blog material is marked as anecdote throughout, and none of it is presented as established practice. That includes the IBC lifetime threads ([TractorByNet](https://tractorbynet.com/forums/goto/post?id=2710792)), Murray Hallam's grow-bed reports ([ecofilms](https://www.ecofilms.com.au/aquaponics-when-growbeds-get-old-sht-happens/)) and the DIY 12 V battery backup, where an old battery lasted about 2 h ([ecofilms battery backup](https://www.ecofilms.com.au/?p=3803)).

## Category summaries

**Fish tanks and sumps.** The main options are IBC totes, round PE/FRP rearing tanks, FRP tanks (India), PVC-tarpaulin biofloc tanks (India), lined concrete or timber tanks, and a sump.

- Netherlands: IBCs cost EUR 59-115 used and EUR 195-205 new (both excl. BTW, binqer.nl), and EUR 369 incl. BTW for a new UV-stabilised food-grade unit (Hornbach).
- India: Indian FRP tanks are listed at INR 18,000-25,000 in Tamil Nadu and INR 10,000-15,500 in Telangana, where the listings conflict. Tarpaulin tanks are INR 4,100-10,000 in Andhra Pradesh.
- US Virgin Islands: the UVI rearing tank costs USD 2,155 and the sump USD 415 (2014).
- Most-mentioned alternative: the IBC tote.
- Gaps:
  - No NL price for PE or FRP round tanks.
  - No Karnataka tank listing.
  - Tank lifetimes come only from UVI depreciation and IBC anecdotes.

**Grow units.** The main options are DWC raft troughs with liner and EPS rafts, NFT channels, media beds, expanded clay or gravel media, vertical towers, Dutch buckets and ebb-and-flow tables.

- Netherlands: pond liners cost EUR 2.69-4.92/m² for 0.5 mm PVC, EUR 7.83-12.88 for 1.0 mm PVC and EUR 12.63-18.46 for 0.6 mm EPDM (Hornbach, incl. BTW).
- India: IndiaMART lists NFT channels at INR 112-1,500 per piece (INR 310 in Chennai), towers at INR 5,400-9,999 and Dutch buckets at INR 175. All are outside the four target states except the Chennai channel.
- US Virgin Islands: UVI rafts cost USD 35.59 each (2014).
- Most-mentioned alternative: homemade insulation-board rafts, which also have the strongest failure evidence (USDA NIFA).
- Gaps:
  - No NL price for raft board, NFT gully, LECA, net pots, buckets or towers.
  - No grow-unit price in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh or Telangana.
  - Ebb-and-flow tables have only FAO geometry.

**Water pumps.** The main options are submersible pond pumps, external centrifugal pumps, engine-driven transfer pumps and airlifts.

**Aeration and backup oxygen.** The main options are piston and linear-diaphragm air pumps, regenerative blowers, diffusers, venturi and jet aerators, cylinder-oxygen backup and AC/DC battery air pumps.

## Inventory tables

Each table below has one row per item and market, and the rows match the JSONL lines one for one. Reading conventions:

- **Systems / methods.** CA is coupled aquaponics, DA decoupled aquaponics and RH recirculating hydroponics. DWC is deep water culture (raft), NFT nutrient film technique, MB media bed, DB Dutch bucket or drip, VT vertical tower and EF ebb and flow.
- **Scale.** H is hobby (under 20 m²), SC small commercial (20-500 m²) and C commercial (over 500 m²). "all" means every value in the list applies.
- **Inferred fit.** System, grow-method and scale fit are agent inferences from the source context unless the sizing cell quotes a rule.
- **Markets.**
  - NL, EU (an EU-wide web store), EU-DE, EU-SI and UK are European rows.
  - IN-KA, IN-TN, IN-AP and IN-TS are Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. IN-national is a national norm or MRP. IN-other is an Indian seller outside the four target states.
  - US is a US extension or literature price. US-VI is the 2014 UVI costing on St Croix.
  - AU is one Australian blog.
  - global means the row is a specification or rule with no price.
- **Price spans.** A price span is the lowest and highest listed value, not a market range.
- **Missing values.** "No price" means the price was not found or not reached; the gap list says which. "-" means not recorded.
- **Repeats.** "as above" repeats the previous row of the same item.
- **Lifetimes.** Each lifetime keeps its basis (depreciation, maker claim, warranty, cycles, hours or anecdote). Hours and cycles are not converted to years.
- **Tier, confidence and corroboration.** These apply to the row's main value, usually its price. Where a note gave a two-level rating ("medium-high", for example FAO 589 read on the FarmHub mirror or the JDK datasheet, or "low-medium", for example the IndiaMART aerator page or Olafs 2014), the row records the lower level (medium or low). The confidence reason does not repeat this.
- **Date read.** All sources were read on 2026-11-25.
- **Status.** All rows are candidate data pending Ajit's review, not canon.

## Conclusion

The inventory is usable now as a **parts list and a rule book, not as a price book**. Three price sets are internally consistent enough to read side by side:

- the UVI costing (US Virgin Islands, 2014 prices);
- the Bluelab EU store ladder, from a EUR 86.40 pen to a EUR 2,625.60 dosing pump;
- the Indian NHB/MIDH structure norms.

Everything else is one listing from one seller on one day, often with VAT or GST unstated. Under AGENTS.md section 1C, any of these values could enter a scenario estimate only after you review and approve it as a manual input. Even then, the estimate would have to stay labelled "scenario estimate, not validated". The inventory also cannot support an energy or labour picture yet. Only 9 rows have a single numeric rated wattage. Another 61 rows are passive at 0 W, and the rest give power only as text, ranges or horsepower in the sizing note. Duty hours exist for one row (the Ecofrost cold room, which "runs constantly"), and minutes per maintenance task exist for none. Electricity cost pressure therefore cannot be derived from it without further reviewed inputs.

The fastest way to raise confidence is to open the leads already in hand. Four priorities stand out:

1. Find a second independent seller for each Netherlands item, so that single-source rows can become corroborated.
2. Extract the MIDH 2025 Annexure V tables, the Telangana Component 7 tables and the Andhra Pradesh guideline. These hold the Indian state rates that are currently missing for Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.
3. Read Dutch glasshouse, heating and LED cost sources (KWIN, the Blueterra heat-pump report, the Berenschot tariff study).
4. Price DO meters, dosing chemicals and oxygen cylinders.

**Two tier decisions need your review before any of this is promoted.** First, FAO 589 and SRAC 454 are recorded as tier_1 here, although two notes recorded them as tier_2. Second, every forum or blog claim in the inventory is labelled as anecdote, and none is presented as established practice.
