# Architecture-Specific CAPEX Basis and Maintenance Driver Research Pack for Media Bed, DWC, and NFT Aquaponics

## Executive summary

The strongest canonical first-pass basis choices are not the same across the three architectures. For **media beds**, the best primary grow-unit CAPEX basis is **`media_volume_m3`**, with `grow_area_m2` as a secondary reporting basis. That choice is supported by aquaponics sources that size simple flood-and-drain systems by **media-bed volume relative to fish-tank volume**, describe media depth as a key design variable, and emphasise that the media itself is doing multiple jobs at once: root support, solids capture, biofiltration, thermal moderation, and air-space provision. Those same sources also show why volume matters operationally: gravel/media weight drives structural requirements, and clogging/cleaning burdens are tied to the amount of media present, not just to surface area. citeturn26view1turn26view0turn26view3turn16view0turn34view0

For **DWC / raft**, the strongest primary grow-unit CAPEX basis is **`raft_surface_area_m2`**, with **`hydro_volume_m3`** as the most useful secondary basis. Raft systems are physically laid out as troughs or ponds sized by raft dimensions and covered water surface; plant capacity, raft count, hole patterns, and handling all track that surface area. At the same time, DWC performance and support burdens are affected by water depth/volume because large water volume acts as a thermal buffer and because aeration and circulation requirements are driven partly by the amount of water being oxygenated and mixed. citeturn33view0turn27view0turn23view0turn19view0

For **NFT**, the most defensible primary grow-unit CAPEX basis is **`channel_length_m`**, with `plant_site_count` and `channel_count` as secondary bases. The strongest evidence here is architectural rather than economic: NFT is described as a system of channels/troughs with specified widths, lengths, slopes, manifolds, and hole spacing. The maintenance burden also follows channel runs and manifolds. In aquaponics, NFT’s system-level burden is materially higher than its channel geometry alone suggests because NFT requires better solids control, may require a separate biofilter, and is more sensitive to pump or flow interruptions than DWC. citeturn17view0turn20view1turn33view4turn36view2turn22view0

A key context split should remain explicit in the business layer: **DWC is not universally “simpler” or “cheaper,” and NFT is not universally “better” or “worse.”** Relative to small media-bed aquaponics, DWC is usually more component-heavy because it adds filtration and aeration; relative to many hydroponic systems, DWC can be mechanically straightforward and labour-efficient because rafts move through the pond and harvest is simple. Likewise, NFT is lightweight and space-efficient, but aquaponic sources consistently describe it as more filtration-sensitive and more failure-sensitive than raft systems. These are **context splits**, not universal business rules. citeturn17view1turn19view0turn27view0turn30search8turn33view4turn21search8

Hydroponic sources were used only where the physical grow-unit architecture is effectively the same in aquaponics and hydroponics. The strongest transferred facts are about **DWC pond/raft geometry, DWC aeration dependence, NFT channel geometry, NFT slope/flow monitoring, and NFT sensitivity to pump interruption**. That transfer is reasonable because the same channels, rafts, emitters, slopes, and root-zone oxygen mechanics are involved. The remaining uncertainty is that aquaponic water carries **more solids, biofilm pressure, and biological contamination pathways**, so hydroponic maintenance frequencies should not be treated as canonical numeric defaults for aquaponics. citeturn23view0turn22view0turn8view1

## Comparative summary

**Media beds** fit the business layer best when treated as a **media-heavy, multi-function grow unit**. Their economic structure is dominated less by “bench area” and more by the fact that the same unit is also carrying media mass, root support, partial solids capture, and biofiltration duty. That is why media volume is the strongest canonical grow-unit basis. Small-scale evidence also shows why media beds are attractive early: they are easy and inexpensive to set up, and in simple configurations the bed itself can serve as solids filter and biofilter. But the same sources identify the scaling penalties: heavy support structures, clogging risk, harder cleaning, anaerobic zones if neglected, and crop-turn labour when roots must be pulled and media reused. citeturn16view0turn18view4turn26view0turn34view0turn30search8

**DWC / raft** fit the business layer best when treated as a **surface-area-based leafy-green platform with volume-linked support requirements**. Raft systems are the most natural fit for leafy greens and herbs in aquaponics, and several institutional sources describe them as the dominant commercial method in aquaponic leafy-greens production. Raft boards, trough dimensions, plant-site density, and harvest handling are all expressed naturally in terms of raft footprint and bed surface. At the same time, hydro volume matters because DWC systems use large water volumes, benefit from that thermal mass, and require effective aeration and circulation. In other words, `raft_surface_area_m2` should drive grow-unit CAPEX, while `hydro_volume_m3` should remain available for aeration, mixing, and disease-risk modelling. citeturn33view0turn27view0turn23view0turn19view2

**NFT** fit the business layer best when treated as a **channel network**, not just as a plant-area surface. The architecture is explicitly a set of narrow channels, laid out by slope and run length, fed by manifolds and emitters, with plant sites cut at spacing intervals. That makes `channel_length_m` the most defensible canonical CAPEX basis. Plant sites matter commercially, but they are a derived production-capacity basis rather than the strongest first-order architecture basis. This matters because two NFT layouts can support similar site counts with different maintenance and failure profiles depending on channel count, length, slope, and manifold complexity. Aquaponic sources also make clear that NFT imposes stronger supporting-system discipline than raft: solids must be removed before the channels, separate biofiltration may be required, and disruption in water flow can lead quickly to wilting and death. citeturn17view0turn20view1turn33view4turn36view2turn22view0

The strongest comparative business lesson is therefore structural: **media beds concentrate grow and filtration functions into one heavy unit; DWC concentrates crop capacity into covered water surface with strong aeration needs; NFT concentrates crop capacity into channel length but externalises filtration and outage risk into support systems.** That is a business-layer schema distinction, not merely an agronomy note. citeturn18view4turn33view1turn36view2turn2view3

## Schema implications

### `data/knowledge/business/capex_models.json`

The cleanest canonical move is to store **primary grow-unit basis** separately from **support-system basis** and from **shared system CAPEX exclusions**. The evidence supports the following mapping as a first-pass canonical default:

- `media_bed.primary_basis = media_volume_m3`  
  `media_bed.secondary_basis = [grow_area_m2, bed_count]`  
  `media_bed.reason = "media is architecture-specific structure + solids capture + biofilter substrate; support load and cleaning scale with media amount"`  
  This is an evidence-backed inference from volume-based sizing, bed-depth guidance, and media-function descriptions. citeturn26view1turn26view0turn26view3turn16view0

- `dwc_raft.primary_basis = raft_surface_area_m2`  
  `dwc_raft.secondary_basis = [hydro_volume_m3, raft_count, plant_site_count]`  
  `dwc_raft.reason = "physical units, plant capacity, board count, and handling all follow raft/trough surface; hydro volume matters for aeration and buffering"`  
  This mirrors how raft systems are described and dimensioned in SRAC, UKY, VT, and Oregon State sources. citeturn33view0turn27view0turn23view0turn19view0

- `nft.primary_basis = channel_length_m`  
  `nft.secondary_basis = [channel_count, plant_site_count, grow_area_m2]`  
  `nft.reason = "channels are the bought/installed/maintained grow-unit; site count is derived from spacing and layout"`  
  This follows the way NFT systems are defined by channels, slopes, run lengths, manifolds, and site spacing. citeturn17view0turn20view1turn33view4

A second schema implication is that **architecture-induced support packages** should not be flattened into the grow-unit basis:

- Media beds may carry an **integrated filtration offset** in simple systems, but that offset should remain conditional because overload can force separate solids removal anyway. citeturn18view4turn36view2  
- DWC support packages should be modelled primarily through **aeration / circulation** and **filtration** bases, not by inflating raft-area unit cost alone. citeturn23view0turn16view3turn36view0  
- NFT support packages should explicitly recognise **fine-solids filtration** and often **separate biofiltration** as architecture-induced requirements. citeturn2view3turn36view2turn33view4

### `data/knowledge/business/cost_models.json`

Recurring non-labour cost models should track **architecture-specific consumables and service burdens**, not just electricity or generic maintenance:

- **Media bed**: media top-up/replacement, pipe/siphon parts, root/solids clean-out consumables, optional worm or biological housekeeping inputs where used. The strongest recurring structure is that media-bed cost burden rises with obstruction management and periodic cleanup rather than with specialised sanitation materials. citeturn16view0turn34view1
- **DWC / raft**: raft replacement or refurbishment, cleaning/disinfection materials, screens/barriers, disease-control inputs, and aeration wear items. e-GRO’s raft sanitation guidance makes disinfection and raft material choice economically relevant rather than purely agronomic. citeturn8view1turn27view0
- **NFT**: emitter/manifold replacement, channel flushing/cleaning supplies, filter media or polishing consumables, and backup-power / outage-readiness consumables where the business layer already models reliability costs. NFT’s small orifices and flow dependence make these architecture-specific recurring costs more defensible than generic “maintenance” buckets. citeturn33view5turn22view0turn21search8

A practical rule for canon is to keep **shared fish-side consumables** separate unless the architecture itself is the reason an extra support stage exists. SRAC explains that solids-removal device choice depends primarily on **organic loading rate** and only secondarily on plant area, which argues against assigning all filtration cost directly to plant architecture. citeturn36view0

### `data/knowledge/business/labor_models.json`

The labour layer should encode each task with a **task class** and a **preferred driver basis**:

- **Architecture-intrinsic**  
  - Media bed: inspect flood-drain devices, clear drain/root obstructions, handle media during crop turns. citeturn9view1turn34view1  
  - DWC: inspect aeration/circulation, move rafts, clean/disinfect rafts. citeturn33view3turn23view0turn8view1  
  - NFT: inspect slope/flow, inspect manifolds and emitters, flush channels, clear roots after crop. citeturn22view0turn33view4turn33view5

- **Architecture-amplified**  
  - Media bed: deep cleaning under high solids loading; blockages from retained roots and organics. citeturn34view0turn36view2  
  - DWC: raft sanitation and disease-response effort under warm, biologically active recirculating conditions. citeturn8view1turn33view1  
  - NFT: filtration policing and outage response because micro-orifices and thin films create brittleness. citeturn33view5turn22view0

- **Crop / management-specific**  
  Trellising, varietal spacing changes, speciality harvest handling, and biosecurity intensity should not be embedded as architecture defaults. Those are strongly affected by crop class, stocking density, climate, and management regime. citeturn16view4turn27view0

A defensible first-pass labour-basis pattern is:

- daily inspection and monitoring → **per operating day per grow unit**  
- cleaning, unclogging, failure response → **per event**  
- post-harvest root clearing / raft sanitation / media turnover → **per crop cycle**  
- heavy substrate handling → **per media_volume_m3** where substrate amounts materially differ  
- channel service → **per channel_length_m** or **per channel**  
- raft handling → **per raft** or **per raft_surface_area_m2** depending on whether the model is physically or commercially oriented. citeturn34view1turn33view3turn33view5turn23view0

## Better decided by simulation later

The following items have strong directional evidence but should **not** be flattened into universal business-layer constants:

- **Outage-damage kinetics.** NFT is the most brittle, media beds can wilt quickly once drained, and DWC is the most buffered, but exact damage timing depends on crop, root mass, temperature, humidity, and whether residual moisture remains in the system. citeturn33view2turn34view0turn22view0
- **Solids-driven cleaning frequency.** The direction is clear across all three architectures, but actual burden varies with fish loading, feed input, solids capture upstream, and management quality. citeturn36view0turn36view2turn31search2
- **Universal DWC-versus-NFT productivity effects.** There is enough evidence to treat NFT as higher risk for aquaponic clogging and filtration, but not enough to encode a universal yield penalty across all crops and designs. citeturn2view6turn2view7
- **Exact labour frequencies.** Weekly versus per-cycle sanitation burdens should be calibrated later against intended operating style rather than baked into canon prematurely. citeturn9view1turn8view1

## Open schema-choice TODOs still remaining

- **Whether `plant_site_count` should become a cross-architecture derived reporting basis.** The evidence strongly supports sites as a useful secondary capacity basis for DWC and NFT, but not as the strongest architecture basis. For media beds it is even less stable because crop flexibility is higher. Keep this as a **schema_choice_todo**, not as the primary basis. citeturn27view0turn20view1turn26view0
- **Whether DWC labour should be driven by `raft_count` or `raft_surface_area_m2`.** The evidence says rafts are real handling units, but large and small rafts can have similar area with different labour ergonomics. If the current schema can store one primary and one fallback basis, use `raft_count` operationally and `raft_surface_area_m2` financially. If it cannot, keep this open. citeturn27view0turn33view3
- **Whether media-bed deep cleaning should be per bed or per media volume.** Volume is structurally stronger, but where bed modules are standardised the simpler `per_bed_per_cycle` model may be good enough. This is a schema-choice issue, not an evidence contradiction. citeturn26view0turn34view1
- **How to allocate architecture-induced support systems in hybrid designs.** Media beds may be used as solids filters in hybrids, while DWC/NFT add downstream plant architectures. The evidence supports keeping these support items separately identifiable, but allocation rules between plant architectures should remain open if the current schema does not already support proportionate sharing. citeturn26view1turn2view3turn30search8
- **Whether NFT backup-power readiness belongs in business canon or only in simulation/risk models.** The structural case is strong because NFT is highly outage-sensitive, but the exact cost treatment depends on how your business layer currently separates maintenance, reliability, and risk penalties. citeturn22view0turn33view4
